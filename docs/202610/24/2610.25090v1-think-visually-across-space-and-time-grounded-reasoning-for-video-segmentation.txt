ABSTRACT 

Video Reasoning Segmentation (VRS) requires a model to identify an implicitly described target and predict its pixel-level mask trajectory, while resolving the spatial relations and temporal events that determine the answer. Text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) can explain a target choice without making the underlying visual references explicit, leaving a video reference gap between linguistic inference and the evidence distributed across frames. We introduce Temporal Chain of Video Primitives (TCoVP) , a spatiotemporally grounded form of Video CoT that organizes query-relevant observations into a chronological reasoning chain. Each primitive associates an entity reference with temporal support and timestamped spatial evidence, and the chain interleaves these references with language to guide reasoning about which entity is relevant, when the queried event holds, and where its evidence appears. The selected target primitives then provide box prompts and temporal support for video mask prediction. We construct TCoVP-SFT-15k and TCoVP-RL-13k to learn this representation through supervised fine-tuning and chain-aligned reinforcement learning. Extensive experiments across five VRS benchmarks and three model backbones demonstrate the strong performance of TCoVP, highlighting the effectiveness of organizing spatial and temporal evidence within the reasoning process. All models and code will be publicly released. Project page: https://victor-wjw.github.io/TCoVP/ 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Video Reasoning Segmentation (VRS) (Yan et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2024) asks a model to resolve an implicit natural-language query into the mask trajectory of its intended target. Because the target may be specified through an interaction, an event sequence, or a transient action, solving the task requires connecting semantic interpretation with visual evidence across space and time. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning offers a way to make these intermediate interpretations explicit, and recent VRS methods use it to reason about a query before producing segmentation prompts (Gong et al., 2026; Wen et al., 2026). The central challenge is to ground the reasoning itself in the evolving scene, so that the entities and events used to identify a target remain tied to the observations that support them. Existing approaches connect language to masks through latent segmentation tokens (Zheng et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2024) or through keyframe selection and geometric prompts (Gong et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026). For VRS, establishing this connection requires distinguishing the entities that provide contextual evidence from the target to segment, while separating the queried action from the longer period during which an object is visible. In Figure 1, the query asks who clasps hands with the athlete with dyed hair before hugging that athlete. The athlete provides the identifying context, whereas the coach involved in the hand-clasp is the target. As the coach remains visible beyond the hand-clasp, attending to the later hug or propagating masks over the full video can produce the wrong answer. This example illustrates the video reference gap : linguistic descriptions can leave unresolved which visual observations support a target choice and when that choice satisfies the query. Spatial references specify the participants in an interaction, while timestamps and temporal support distinguish suc-cessive events and delimit the action of interest. Interleaving these references with language gives subsequent reasoning steps access to explicitly localized evidence and makes intermediate grounding available for supervision. The same representation also connects the resulting target decision to the spatial prompts and temporal support needed for mask prediction. We realize this idea through Temporal Chain of Video Primitives (TCoVP) , which represents query-conditioned visual evidence as a chronological chain of timestamped video primitives. Each primitive combines an entity reference, a temporal span, and a bounding box at an observed times-tamp, together with a semantic description. Entity identifiers link references within the generated chain, while chronological ordering records how the supporting observations unfold over the video. A canonical serialization interleaves these primitives with natural-language reasoning, allowing contextual observations and target evidence to be expressed in a common form. The selected target primitives supply timestamped box prompts for mask propagation, and their temporal support restricts the output when the query specifies an action interval. We train TCoVP through supervised fine-tuning on TCoVP-SFT-15k and chain-aligned reinforcement learning on TCoVP-RL-13k , with rewards for structural validity, temporal alignment, spatiotemporal grounding, and target primitive accuracy. Evaluation spans five VRS benchmarks and Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, and Molmo2 backbones. With Qwen2.5-VL-3B and the data volume fixed, TCoVP and its intermediate grounding supervision improve the five-benchmark macro-average J &F from 57.8 to 62.7 over text-only CoT with the same answer-primitive format. The largest gain of 7.4 points occurs on GroundMoRe, where queries require explicit temporal event localization. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We formulate the video reference gap and introduce Temporal Chain of Video Primitives (TCoVP) , which constructs entity identity and timestamped observations progressively over the video in a single reasoning trace, and makes the selected target primitives executable for VRS. • We construct TCoVP-SFT-15k and TCoVP-RL-13k and train TCoVP with chain-aligned rewards for structural validity, temporal alignment, spatiotemporal grounding, and target primitive accuracy. • We evaluate TCoVP across five VRS benchmarks and multiple MLLM backbones. Controlled comparisons show consistent improvements over text-only CoT, especially on queries that require explicit temporal event localization. 22 RELATED WORK 

Video Reasoning Segmentation. VRS extends referring video segmentation from explicit object descriptions to queries that encode intent, commonsense, and temporal logic (Yan et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2024). Supervised approaches map MLLM representations to segmentation tokens or mask decoders (Zheng et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Dai et al., 2025). RL-based methods instead optimize intermediate reasoning, keyframe selection, or spatial grounding (Gong et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025; Wen et al., 2026). VideoSEG-O3 uses multi-turn tool interaction to refine temporal and spatial predictions (Dai et al., 2026), while VIRST fuses video-language and segmentation representations in a unified model (Hong & Do, 2026). TCoVP targets a complementary interface problem: it makes references to relevant entities and events explicit through a single-turn temporally ordered chain of video primitives before dense mask propagation. 

Visual primitives and grounded video reasoning. Thinking-with-image methods manipulate crops, regions, or visual tokens during reasoning (OpenAI, 2025; Zheng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a;b; Fan et al., 2025). Thinking with Visual Primitives instead treats points and boxes as units of image reasoning that close the reference gap between linguistic thoughts and visual entities (Lu et al., 2026). Video introduces temporal structure to this gap. Open-o3 Video generates timestamped spatial evidence for video question answering (Meng et al., 2025), STVG-o1 produces bounding-box CoT for spatio-temporal grounding (Gu et al., 2025), and EventCoT grounds answers in selected temporal events (Song et al., 2026). TCoVP focuses on implicit-query segmentation and organizes visual evidence as a chronological chain of timestamped video primitives. This structure distinguishes entity identity from individual observations, represents their temporal ordering, and exposes the selected target primitives to a video mask propagator.

Existing video object segmentation datasets provide masks for target objects, but they do not directly supervise a model to use visual references during reasoning. In particular, they rarely associate target and contextual entities with temporal supports and timestamped spatial evidence, even when these entities and their interactions determine the answer to an implicit query. A language-only trace therefore cannot be checked for entity identity, event timing, or spatial grounding. To address this limitation, we construct supervision in the form of chronologically ordered chains of timestamped video primitives. TCoVP-RL-13k retains examples with reward-compatible video primitive annotations for reinforce-ment learning. Our datasets are constructed on top of the training splits of existing video object segmentation datasets, including ReVOS (Yan et al., 2024), MeViS (Ding et al., 2023), Long-RVOS (Liang et al., 2026), and GroundMoRe (Deng et al., 2025), and cover single-target, multi-target, and zero-target samples. Specifically, we design the following automated data annotation pipeline: 

Data preprocessing. We uniformly sample 16 frames from each video while always retaining the first and last frames. For samples with annotated targets, we remove instances whose target is absent from every sampled frame. Target masks are converted into boxes at the sampled timestamps. The frame with the largest visible target region supplies the spatial evidence used in the expected answer, while the remaining sampled frames provide evidence for contextual primitives. 

Temporal-chain annotation. Kimi-K2.5 (Team et al., 2026) generates an initial reasoning trace from the sampled video and task annotations. Whenever the trace refers to a visually relevant entity, it emits a video primitive containing an entity identifier, temporal support, a detected keyframe timestamp, and a bounding box. Subsequent observations of the same entity reuse its identifier, thereby preserving entity continuity across time. The emitted primitives follow the chronological order of their keyframe timestamps. The trace typically starts from the earliest available evidence and progresses along the video, while allowing revisits to earlier primitives when a later event changes their interpretation. The resulting serialization interleaves natural-language reasoning with temporally ordered primitive records. We retain a text-only counterpart to isolate the effect of the temporal-primitive representation. 

Chain consistency and primitive filtering. We parse every serialized primitive and require it to contain a valid entity identifier, temporal support, detected keyframe timestamp, and bounding box. We additionally check timestamp order and consistent reuse of entity identifiers across observations. References that cannot be matched to an annotated entity are removed, and samples with invalid or unresolved target primitives are discarded. Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Bai et al., 2025b) performs a second-stage semantic check on bounding boxes for contextual entities. This pipeline converts mask-oriented VRS data into supervision for spatiotemporally grounded referential reasoning, as summarized in Figure 2. 3.3 TRAINING STRATEGY

4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

We instantiate TCoVP with Qwen2.5-VL-3B (Bai et al., 2025b), Qwen3-VL-4B (Bai et al., 2025a), and Molmo2-4B (Clark et al., 2026). All models use 16 uniformly sampled frames, one SFT epoch, two reinforcement-learning epochs, and eight sampled responses per query. The Qwen models use LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) for SFT with a learning rate of 4 × 10 −5 and a reinforcement-learning rate of 

5 × 10 −7. The Molmo2 models use full-parameter SFT at 2 × 10 −6 and reinforcement learning at 

2 × 10 −7. Target primitives in the final answer block provide timestamped bounding-box prompts to SAM3 for video mask propagation. 4.2 EVALUATION DATASETS AND METRICS 

We evaluate on Long-RVOS (Liang et al., 2026), ReasonVOS (Bai et al., 2024), ReVOS (Yan et al., 2024), MeViS (Ding et al., 2023), and the motion-grounded GroundMoRe benchmark (Deng et al., 2025). Following prior VRS protocols (Yan et al., 2024), we report region similarity J , contour accuracy F, and their average J &F. The first four benchmarks test target resolution from implicit video queries, while GroundMoRe additionally requires the model to distinguish the action interval that satisfies the query from the longer interval in which an entity is merely visible. 4.3 MAIN RESULTS 

Motion-grounded VRS. GroundMoRe evaluates the temporal component of the video reference gap, as the target is defined by an action or relation that holds over a bounded interval. All three variants outperform the strongest listed prior VRS baseline, which scores 31.96. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of TCoVP on queries that require identifying the target together with its relevant action interval. 

General VRS performance. Table 2 shows that TCoVP performs well across multiple MLLM backbones. All three variants consistently outperform the strongest listed prior baseline on Long-RVOS and ReasonVOS, with gains of 7.7 to 10.0 and 6.7 to 7.3 points, respectively. 4.4 ABLATION STUDIES 

All ablation studies below use TCoVP-3B instantiated with Qwen2.5-VL-3B (Bai et al., 2025b) to examine the reasoning representation, the reinforcement-learning stage, and the temporal alignment and spatiotemporal grounding rewards. Additional experiments are provided in Appendix A.2. 

TCoVP versus text-only CoT. Table 3 keeps the data volume, training configuration, and backbone fixed while varying the reasoning representation. TCoVP raises the five-benchmark macro average from 57.8 to 62.7, with a gain of 7.4 points on GroundMoRe and gains ranging from 2.5 to 6.4 points on the other benchmarks. Relative to Veason-R1, the macro average increases from 48.3 to 62.7. Incorporating explicit temporal and spatial references into intermediate reasoning, together with their grounding supervision, improves segmentation under the same backbone and data budget. 

Effect of chain-aligned reinforcement learning. Adding chain-aligned reinforcement learning to SFT consistently improves performance across all four benchmarks, yielding gains of 8.8, 8.5, 5.4, and 9.9 points on Long-RVOS, ReasonVOS, ReVOS, and GroundMoRe, respectively. Chain-aligned reinforcement learning therefore provides substantial gains beyond supervised fine-tuning, with the largest improvement on GroundMoRe. 

Effect of temporal alignment and spatiotemporal grounding rewards. Removing either Rta 

or Rstg decreases performance across all four benchmarks. Using both rewards yields the best performance across all four benchmarks, indicating that each contributes to the final segmentation quality. 

5 LIMITATIONS 

TCoVP reasons over 16 uniformly sampled frames, so brief events may occur between observations and remain unobserved. Although the temporal primitive chain provides explicit entity references and spatiotemporal evidence, cross-entity actions and relations are expressed in natural language without a dedicated structured representation. The final masks also inherit errors from SAM3 propagation. Extending TCoVP to more complex video scenarios may require adaptive temporal sampling, denser spatial evidence, and structured representations of interactions. 96 CONCLUSION 

We introduce TCoVP, a reasoning framework that extends primitive-based visual reasoning to videos through a temporal chain of video primitives. The chain accumulates query-relevant evidence in chronological order, while repeated entity identifiers link observations across time. Each primitive provides explicit temporal support and spatial grounding, making intermediate reasoning structured and verifiable. Interleaving these primitives with language enables autoregressive MLLMs to generate grounded reasoning traces, whose final target primitives provide timestamped bounding-box prompts to SAM3 for mask propagation. Improvements across multiple backbones and benchmarks, partic-ularly on motion-grounded queries, support the value of maintaining entity continuity and explicit temporal and spatial references throughout video reasoning. At inference time, we uniformly sample 16 frames from each video and apply the same model-specific image-pixel constraints as during training: 128 × 28 × 28 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 128 × 32 × 32 

for Qwen3-VL-4B and Molmo2-4B. We use the same system prompt as in training and perform MLLM inference with vLLM to generate the TCoVP reasoning trace and final answer. We then parse the target primitives in the <answer> block and extract their timestamps and bounding boxes as prompts for SAM3 (Carion et al., 2025). Each timestamped box initializes a mask track on the corresponding video frame, and the resulting tracks are propagated through the video and merged by pixel-wise union. For GroundMoRe, mask propagation is restricted to the predicted query-relevant action interval; if the answer contains no valid target primitive, the model returns an empty mask trajectory. A.3 MORE QUALITATIVE COMPARISONS AND FAILURE CASE ANALYSIS 

A.3.1 COMPARISON WITH VIDEO SEG-O3-4B Figure 7 compares TCoVP-4B with VideoSEG-O3-4B (Dai et al., 2026) on a query that asks what the man in white misses after dribbling between the legs of the police officer. VideoSEG-O3-4B uses a two-round procedure: it first analyzes a coarse set of temporal and spatial frames to identify the relevant action segment, and then refines the segment using fewer frames and a selected keyframe. Although this procedure localizes the shot attempt, it does not explicitly represent the identity, temporal support, and spatial trajectory of the queried object. Its final mask is therefore not tightly aligned with the basketball. In contrast, TCoVP-4B grounds the contextual participants and the basketball as separate entities. It identifies the relevant interval as 9.3–11.7 seconds and provides timestamped bounding boxes for the basketball at multiple moments during the shot attempt. The resulting answer primitive directly refers to the basketball and supplies a temporally and spatially localized prompt for mask propagation.
