## ABSTRACT 

Increasing the attention context length significantly improves streaming video generation quality, while incurring prohibitive memory and computational costs as the context grows. Existing methods attempt to capture this contextual infor-mation through attention selection or compression, but remain limited by bounded representations and lack explicit attention-level supervision to retain historical contributions to current queries. To address this, we introduce Attn-Forcing , a novel framework that preserves full-context attention contributions for streaming video generation under bounded memory and computation. We achieve this by in-troducing attention proxies that continuously approximate the attention responses induced by the historical context. Specifically, we implement the proxies as learn-able visual latents and employ a Proxy Update Module to incorporate visual con-text from historical latents. Moreover, we propose an Attention Matching Loss to explicitly constrain the attention behavior of the learned proxies using full-context attention supervision. Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments demon-strate that Attn-Forcing consistently improves streaming video generation quality and achieves state-of-the-art performance over existing baseline methods. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Streaming video generation has gained increasing attention due to its broad applications in interac-tive content creation, digital humans, live streaming, and persistent virtual environments. With re-cent advances in autoregressive (AR) diffusion models (Jin et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025b; Yuan et al., 2026; Teng et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2026a), streaming generation is commonly im-plemented through chunk-by-chunk generation with causal KV caching in Diffusion Transformers (DiT) (Peebles & Xie, 2023). Existing methods (Huang et al., 2026; Zhu et al., 2026; Yi et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2025a) typically retain only a limited-length KV cache during training and inference. This trunca-tion of full-context attention discards contributions from historical KV states, leading to degraded generation quality. As shown by the experiment in Figure 1, although Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2026) is trained with a fixed local attention window, simply increasing the KV cache length at infer-ence consistently yields higher VBench-Long (Huang et al., 2024) scores, highlighting the benefit of broader historical context. However, under bounded memory and computational budgets, main-taining increasingly long KV contexts is impractical for streaming generation. To address this challenge, Guo et al. (2025); Yi et al. (2025); Ji et al. (2025); Ma et al. (2026b) selec-tively retain or retrieve relevant historical KV states through adaptive strategies, but remain limited by bounded KV budgets and potentially accumulated errors in current visual latents. Another line of work (Wu et al., 2025b; Lu et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026; Xue et al., 2026; Bian et al., 2026) compresses historical context into compact representations using dedicated objectives or pre-defined compression ratios/schedules, but inevitably faces a trade-off between context coverage and information fidelity. Fundamentally, these methods still perform attention over bounded historical representations and lack attention-level supervision, making it difficult to preserve the contribution of the full context to current queries. Can we capture this contribution without explicitly maintaining the full context?

A larger attention win-dow generally improves generation quality, but at the cost of increased peak GPU memory. In this paper, we answer this ques-tion by proposing Attn-Forcing , a novel framework that approximates full-context attention under bounded memory and computation. We achieve this by learning additional attention proxies during distribution matching distillation (DMD) training (Yin et al., 2024). Specifically, we implement the proxies as learnable latents and continuously update them through a Proxy Update Module us-ing visual latents evicted from the lo-cal attention window to incorporate the visual history. We then forward the proxy latents through the DiT and use their layer-wise KV states for subsequent streaming generation. However, the DMD training objec-tive primarily matches the model output distribution, without explic-itly constraining the attention behav-ior induced by the learned proxies. We therefore introduce an Attention Matching Loss to directly match the attention responses obtained with the learned proxies to those of the historical attention. Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments demonstrate that Attn-Forcing achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance over baseline methods. Moreover, ablation studies confirm the effec-tiveness and advantages of proposed designs, providing valuable insights for the streaming video generation community. The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We propose Attn-Forcing, a novel DMD training framework that, for the first time, pre-serves full-context attention contributions under bounded memory and computation during streaming generation. • We introduce learnable attention proxies to continuously incorporate visual history and a novel Attention Matching Loss to further align their attention responses with full-context attention. • Experiments show that Attn-Forcing improves streaming video generation quality and achieves SOTA performance over baseline methods. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 VIDEO DIFFUSION MODELS 

Early video diffusion models typically extend image diffusion backbones with temporal modules to model spatiotemporal dependencies, enabling high-quality text-to-video generation (Ho et al., 2022; Blattmann et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2024). With the emergence of Diffusion Trans-formers (DiT) (Peebles & Xie, 2023), video generation has progressively shifted toward large-scale transformer architectures with stronger scalability and global temporal modeling. Representative models such as Sora (Brooks et al., 2024), CogVideoX (Yang et al., 2025b), MovieGen (Polyak et al., 2024), HunyuanVideo (Kong et al., 2024), and Wan (Wan et al., 2025) have demonstrated im-pressive video generation capabilities, producing high-fidelity videos with coherent motion, strong temporal consistency, and faithful alignment with textual prompts. Recent open-source models, in-cluding LTX-Video (HaCohen et al., 2024), Wan2.2 (Wan et al., 2025), and HunyuanVideo-1.5 (Wu et al., 2025a), further push the performance and efficiency of open-source video generation. Mean-while, frontier systems such as Veo 3.1 (Google DeepMind, 2025), Kling 3.0 (Kuaishou, 2026), 2arxiv Preprint, 2026 Seedance 2.0 (Seedance et al., 2026), MiniMax H3 (MiniMax, 2026), and HappyHorse (Alibaba Cloud, 2026) exhibit increasingly sophisticated generation capabilities, including realistic physical dynamics, complex motion, multimodal conditioning, and joint audio-video generation. Our work is built upon Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (Wan et al., 2025), adapting it for streaming long video generation. 2.2 LONG VIDEO GENERATION 

Long video generation remains challenging for video diffusion models due to the high computational cost of full-sequence modeling and the temporal extrapolation gap between short-video training and long-video inference. One line of work seeks to extrapolate pretrained short-video diffusion models to longer durations without costly long-video retraining. Representative approaches modify the inference process or temporal modeling through noise rescheduling, progressive denoising, or positional extrapolation, including FreeNoise (Qiu et al., 2024), FIFO-Diffusion (Kim et al., 2024), RIFLEx (Zhao et al., 2025), and Diff-VF (Yang et al., 2026). These methods substantially extend the generation horizon of pretrained video diffusion models while improving temporal continuity over long sequences. More recently, autoregressive (AR) video diffusion has emerged as a promising paradigm for stream-ing long video generation. Specifically, CausVid (Yin et al., 2025) introduces causal diffusion distil-lation to enable efficient streaming generation, while Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2026) further trains on self-generated histories to reduce the autoregressive train-test discrepancy. Subsequent works such as LongLive (Yang et al., 2025a) and Self-Forcing++ (Cui et al., 2026) further improve long-horizon generation through dedicated long-rollout training and inference strategies. However, due to GPU memory constraints, these methods are restricted to fixed-length KV caches that continuously discard historical information, resulting in degraded and unstable generation quality. To address this, recent works explore different strategies for preserving useful historical context un-der bounded memory and computation. Resampling Forcing (Guo et al., 2025) and MemFlow (Ji et al., 2025) dynamically retrieve or activate relevant historical states for current generation, while Deep Forcing (Yi et al., 2025) and FlowCache (Ma et al., 2026b) prune or compress KV caches according to their estimated importance. Other approaches (Wu et al., 2025b; Lu et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2026; Xue et al., 2026; Bian et al., 2026) aggregate or compress historical information into compact representations with bounded computational cost. However, these methods still perform attention over bounded historical representations and lack explicit attention-level supervision to pre-serve full-context contributions to current queries. To address this, we introduce attention proxies and train them with an additional Attention Matching loss.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we presented Attn-Forcing, a novel framework for preserving full-context attention contributions in streaming video generation under bounded memory and computation. We achieve this by introducing learnable attention proxies that continuously incorporate evicted visual context and produce native KV states for subsequent generation. To further improve their approximation of historical attention responses, we proposed an Attention Matching Loss with efficient full-context supervision constructed from online accumulated statistics. Extensive experiments on both short-and long-horizon video generation demonstrate that Attn-Forcing consistently improves generation quality and achieves state-of-the-art performance over existing baselines, highlighting the effec-tiveness of explicitly modeling historical attention contributions for high-quality streaming video generation. 9arxiv Preprint, 2026

A.2 TRAINING AND INFERENCE PROCEDURE 

We summarize the training and inference procedures of Attn-Forcing in Algorithms 1 and 2, respec-tively. During streaming long tuning, we first perform standard DMD training until visual latents begin to be evicted from the local attention window. Once eviction occurs, the evicted states are incorporated into our proxy training process. Specifically, the corresponding visual latents are used to update the proxy latents through the Proxy Update Module, while their cached layer-wise KV states are used to update the historical attention statistics. Starting from subsequent chunks, the up-dated proxy latents are propagated through the standard DiT forward pass to obtain their layer-wise proxy KV states, which are then inserted between the sink and local KV states for attention compu-tation. Meanwhile, for the last L = 6 DiT layers, the accumulated statistics are used to construct the historical attention supervision for LAM . The resulting LAM is combined with the DMD objective according to Eq. 17. Although Attn-Forcing preserves historical attention contributions under bounded memory and com-putation, several limitations remain. First, Attn-Forcing maintains a fixed number of attention prox-ies throughout generation. While this design keeps the additional memory and computational over-head bounded, it also imposes a fixed capacity for representing the accumulated history. As the generation horizon grows substantially longer or the visual content becomes increasingly diverse, a limited number of proxies may become insufficient to preserve all relevant historical information. Second, the Attention Matching Loss relies on a random-feature approximation of the historical attention response rather than exact full-context attention. Although this approximation enables efficient long-context supervision using constant-size online statistics, it inevitably introduces ap-proximation error into the supervision signal. 16
