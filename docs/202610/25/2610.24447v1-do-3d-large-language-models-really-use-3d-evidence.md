## 摘要
近期的 3D 大语言模型（3D-LLMs）经过大量后训练，在场景问答上表现出强劲性能。然而，现有基准很少评估 3D-LLMs 如何应对指称歧义、场景证据不足或错误预设，从而难以判断其回答是否真正基于 3D 证据。在本工作中，我们提出 UNICORNS，一个用于评估 3D-LLM 回答在四种证据条件下可靠性的基准。UNICORNS 包含覆盖 557 个 ScanNet 扫描的 11,387 个问题，涵盖八个挑战类别以及可回答的对照问题。只有当回答满足所需响应行为，且不包含任何缺乏可用证据支持的场景断言时，才算通过。对六个开源 3D-LLMs 的评估揭示出明显的性能差距：即使最强的零样本模型在可回答对照问题上达到 61.22% 的准确率，但在八个挑战类别上仅为 40.94%。为弥补这一差距，我们进一步提出 HORN，一种义务感知的后训练方法，结合组相对任务奖励、叶节点条件义务奖励和监督控制回放。大量实验表明，HORN 在挑战类别上达到 75.1 ± 1.0%，总体达到 77.2 ± 0.8%，优于所有基线且不降低可回答对照问题的准确率。我们的发现有助于对 3D-LLMs 进行系统评估并改进其基于证据的 3D 推理（代码：https://anonymous.4open.science/r/unicorns-6B38/）。

## Abstract
Recent 3D Large Language Models (3D-LLMs) have shown strong performance on scene question answering with extensive post-training. Yet, existing benchmarks rarely evaluate how 3D-LLMs respond to referential ambiguity, insufficient scene evidence, or false presuppositions, obscuring whether their responses are grounded in 3D evidence. In this work, we introduce UNICORNS, a benchmark for evaluating the reliability of 3D-LLM responses across four evidence conditions. UNICORNS contains 11,387 questions across 557 ScanNet scans, covering eight challenge categories alongside answerable controls. A response passes only if it satisfies the required response behavior and contains no scene claim unsupported by the available evidence. Evaluation of six open-sourced 3D-LLMs reveals a pronounced performance gap: even the strongest zero-shot model achieves 61.22% accuracy on answerable controls, but only 40.94% across the eight challenge categories. To address this gap, we further propose HORN, an obligation-aware post-training method combining group-relative task rewards, leaf-conditioned obligation rewards, and supervised control replay. Extensive experiments demonstrate that HORN reaches $75.1 \pm 1.0\%$ across the challenge categories and $77.2 \pm 0.8\%$ overall, outperforming all baselines without degrading answerable-control accuracy. Our findings enable a systematic evaluation and improvement of evidence-grounded 3D reasoning for 3D-LLMs (code: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/unicorns-6B38/).
