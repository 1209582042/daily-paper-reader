Abstract
Dynamic representations based on 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS)
have shown strong capabilities in reconstruction and rendering,
yet current methods remain limited in supporting complex natural-
language referring in dynamic scenes. Existing 4D Gaussian meth-
ods are primarily designed for rendering-oriented dynamic model-
ing, while current semantic extensions often rely on scene-specific
semantic optimization or are evaluated on relatively simple query
settings. In this paper, we study Referring 4D Gaussian Splatting

Introduction
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a powerful represen-
tation for static scene reconstruction and novel-view rendering,
offering high visual fidelity and efficient rasterization [13]. Build-
ing on this foundation, recent dynamic Gaussian methods extend
Gaussian primitives across time through deformation, temporal
conditioning, or explicit spacetime modeling, substantially advanc-
ing dynamic scene reconstruction and free-viewpoint rendering
[18, 23, 39, 42, 43]. Among these developments, 4D Gaussian Splat-
ting (4DGS) has emerged as an effective representation for dynamic
scene modeling, enabling high-fidelity rendering with strong effi-
ciency [39, 43]. Together, these advances establish Gaussian splat-
ting as a practical substrate for dynamic visual modeling.
At the same time, recent work has begun to study language
grounding and semantic reasoning on Gaussian representations.
In static 3D scenes, methods such as LangSplat [31], Gaussian
Grouping [44], OpenGaussian [41], Dr. Splat [12], Segment then
Splat [22], and ReferSplat [7] connect Gaussian primitives with
language or segmentation supervision. In dynamic settings, SA4D
[8] and 4D LangSplat [17] extend Gaussian-based semantics to
time-varying scenes. However, many existing methods are opti-
mized for category-level querying, scene-specific semantic fitting,
or mask prediction, and are not designed for expressions involving
temporal state changes, interaction cues, multi-target reasoning, or
deliberately absent targets.
This gap becomes critical when the query involves temporal
states, relations, or set-level constraints. A practical model should
handle expressions such as the cookie broken into smaller pieces, the
complete lemon before the cut, all objects except the hand and the metal
cup, or descriptions with no valid referent. These queries require
temporal reasoning, entity disambiguation, set-level constraints,
and uncertainty-aware rejection. Figure 2 summarizes the transition
from conventional semantic querying to dynamic referring.
Figure 1 provides example visualizations of ReferGaussian for
dynamic and multi-target referring in 4D scenes.
To address these limitations, we define Referring 4D Gaussian
Splatting (R4DGS), which requires a 4D Gaussian system to resolve
complex natural-language referring expressions in dynamic scenes
and produce temporally grounded, set-valued segmentation masks.
Recognizing the lack of a benchmark for this task, we introduce
R4D-Bench-QA (QA denotes Query Annotations), which evaluates

three challenging categories: 4D-varying queries, multi-target or
reasoning queries, and zero-target or distractor queries.
These observations reveal an abstraction gap between low-level
Gaussian primitives and the entity-level units referred to by natu-
ral language. Dynamic Gaussian backbones provide efficient time-
conditioned scene representations, but their primitives are not
directly organized into persistent, language-addressable entities.
Meanwhile, SA4D and 4D LangSplat enrich dynamic scenes with
segmentation identities or learned language fields [8, 17], yet re-
main primarily oriented toward segmentation-centric outputs or
scene-specific semantic fitting. Consequently, temporal-state com-
position, multi-entity selection, and zero-target rejection are not
naturally supported within a unified referring protocol.
Motivated by this gap, we propose ReferGaussian, a training-free
referring framework built upon 4DGS. Starting from a reconstructed
4D Gaussian scene, it uses mask-supported multi-view evidence to
form persistent Gaussian entities, records their appearance, motion,
relations, and valid temporal support in an EntityBank, and resolves
structured referring constraints over this bank. MLLMs serve as
the Refer-Planner for query decomposition and constraint extrac-
tion, while Grounded-SAM supplies 2D mask evidence. The final
prediction is produced through explicit and inspectable entity-level
selection, without scene-specific semantic training.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
(1) We formulate Referring 4D Gaussian Splatting (R4DGS), a
novel task for language-grounded dynamic scene under-
standing involving temporal state changes, multi-target rea-
soning, and distractor rejection.
(2) We establish R4D-Bench-QA, the first benchmark for R4DGS,
featuring structured query annotations and pixel-level spa-
tiotemporal supervision to address the current lack of eval-
uation standards.
(3) We propose ReferGaussian, a training-free framework that
organizes low-level dynamic Gaussians into persistent enti-
ties addressable through language and performs constraint-
aware 4D referring over an EntityBank.

Related Work
Dynamic Scene Reconstruction. NeRF [25] and its dynamic
variants [3, 19, 26, 27, 30] laid the foundation for neural dynamic
view synthesis. 3D Gaussian Splatting [13] replaced volumetric
rendering with explicit anisotropic Gaussian primitives, making
real-time rendering practical. Recent work extends Gaussian splat-
ting to dynamic scenes through deformation, canonicalization, or
explicit spacetime modeling, including Dynamic 3D Gaussians [23],
Deformable 3D Gaussians [42], 4D Gaussian Splatting [39], Real-
time Photorealistic Dynamic Scene Representation and Rendering
with 4D Gaussian Splatting [43], and Spacetime Gaussian Feature
Splatting [18]. These methods deliver strong rendering quality, yet
their objective remains view synthesis. Our target is language-
conditioned reasoning over dynamic entities. This requires tem-
poral support that remains stable across rendering, downstream
grouping, candidate ranking, and set-level decisions under ambigu-
ous natural language. In practice, this difference is critical when
multiple visually similar candidates coexist but only one satisfies
temporal or relational constraints across a full event window.
Language-Grounded Semantic Reasoning. Visual semantic rea-
soning has advanced across various domains. In 2D video un-
derstanding, several works explore semantic tasks such as open-
vocabulary and referring segmentation [9–11, 24, 34, 35, 38, 40]. For
static 3D scenes, current research explores two primary directions:
open-vocabulary understanding and segmentation [5, 6, 12, 14, 22,
28, 29, 31, 41, 44, 45], and referring expression grounding repre-
sented by ReferSplat [7]. In dynamic contexts, while SA4D [8] and
4D LangSplat [17] enable time-sensitive language querying, they
primarily focus on semantic segmentation, with limited coverage
of complex referring understanding. To our knowledge, referring
grounding in dynamic Gaussian scenes has not been systemati-
cally formulated or evaluated. Unlike existing methods that rely
on scene-specific semantic retraining or learned language fields,
ReferGaussian resolves referents through entity-level matching
within a query-reusable structure. This training-free design sup-
ports robust reasoning over temporal states and set-valued targets
through persistent entity records and explicit constraints.
Foundation Models. Large vision-language and segmentation
foundation models provide strong building blocks for language-
grounded scene reasoning. Qwen3-VL [1] supports structured inter-
pretation of natural-language expressions, while Grounding DINO
[21], Segment Anything [15], and Grounded-SAM [33] enable text-
conditioned mask extraction. Recent multimodal systems [4, 20, 37]
further improve prompt following and visual grounding. ReferGaus-
sian uses these models as modular components for query parsing
and visual evidence extraction within a structured entity-centric
pipeline. This decomposition improves diagnostic ability: query
decomposition errors, mask evidence errors, and entity association
errors can be analyzed separately.
3
Task and Benchmark
In this section, we first formalize Referring 4D Gaussian Splatting
(R4DGS) and clarify its task setting. We then introduce R4D-Bench-
QA, our benchmark for dynamic 4D referring understanding, to-
gether with its construction pipeline, annotation protocol, query
taxonomy, and evaluation metrics.
3.1
Referring 4D Gaussian Splatting (R4DGS)
Given a multi-view dynamic video, a precomputed 4D Gaussian
scene representation, and a natural-language query 𝑞, R4DGS iden-
tifies the referred entities and produces their frame-aligned segmen-
tation masks. The referent may be a single entity, multiple entities,
or an empty set, depending on the spatiotemporal, relational, and
set-level constraints expressed by the query.
R4DGS adds three requirements to conventional referring: (1)
Temporally varying queries specify an event stage or a state before
or after a transition. (2) Set-valued and reasoning-intensive queries
express conjunction, exclusion, quantification, or relations among
entities. (3) Zero-target and distractor queries may describe no valid
referent and therefore return an empty prediction.

Benchmark Construction. As illustrated in Figure 3, the bench-
mark is constructed in three stages. In Stage 1, each dynamic video is
processed to obtain temporally associated mask tracks and a vocab-
ulary of trackable object candidates. Segmentation models provide
frame-level mask evidence, while an MLLM assists in identifying
concise object phrases that remain visually traceable throughout
the sequence.
In Stage 2, annotators inspect the mask overlays and divide each
sequence into semantically meaningful temporal segments accord-
ing to object-state changes, interactions, and event boundaries. Key
frames are selected from each segment to summarize its visual
content. Based on the tracked objects and temporal segments, anno-
tators construct entity-centric referring expressions and associate
each expression with its complete target set, valid temporal sup-
port, and frame-aligned masks. Multi-target annotations include
the complete set of entities satisfying the query.
Stage 3 performs query-specific quality control. Temporally vary-
ing queries are checked for unambiguous state descriptions and
valid event boundaries. Multi-target queries are checked for rela-
tional consistency and completeness of the annotated target set.
Zero-target queries are retained only when no annotated entity
satisfies all query constraints. Three expert annotators perform ver-
ification, while MLLM-based checks flag ambiguous or inconsistent
cases for human inspection.
Query Construction and Taxonomy. R4D-Bench-QA covers
three categories: 4D variation, multi-target reasoning, and zero-
target or distractor cases. Together, they span state transitions,
temporal ordering, conjunction, disjunction, exclusion, object inter-
actions, and target-cardinality constraints. Query wording is varied
through attributes, relational phrases, event descriptions, and refer-
ring structures while preserving the underlying target semantics.
This controlled variation prevents evaluation from depending on a
narrow set of prompt templates.
Figure 4 summarizes the benchmark vocabulary, query-category
composition, and expression-length distribution. Together, these
statistics show that R4D-Bench-QA contains both concise object ref-
erences and longer compositional expressions involving temporal
or set-level constraints.

4
Method
ReferGaussian addresses the abstraction gap between low-level
dynamic Gaussian primitives and the entity-level units referred
to by natural language. Given a precomputed 4D Gaussian scene,
our method constructs persistent Gaussian entities from multi-
frame visual evidence, organizes them into an EntityBank, and
performs constraint-aware referent selection without optimizing
an additional scene-specific semantic field.

4.2
Framework
Given dynamic Gaussians G, calibrated video frames {I𝑡}, and a
query 𝑞, ReferGaussian follows three semantic stages.
Stage 1: query-guided entity discovery uses an MLLM-based Refer-
Planner to extract trackable subject phrases and coarse temporal
cues. Grounded-SAM2 produces phrase-conditioned mask tracks,
which are iteratively lifted from sampled frames into Gaussian-
supported candidate entities.
Stage 2: EntityBank construction summarizes each candidate using
representative observations, active temporal segments, appearance
and state descriptions, motion cues, and relations. These records
form an inspectable EntityBank over the reconstructed scene.
Stage 3: constraint-aware referent selection validates the structured
query and selects candidate entities and temporal segments under
identity, temporal, relational, cardinality, exclusion, and zero-target
constraints. The selected Gaussian supports are finally rasterized
to produce frame-aligned masks.
Figure 5 illustrates the overall pipeline. This decomposition
changes the reasoning unit from a pixel or frame-wise feature
match to an entity with persistent Gaussian support. Stage 1 estab-
lishes spatial and temporal correspondence between 2D evidence
and 4D primitives, Stage 2 makes the entities language-addressable,
and Stage 3 produces a globally consistent set-valued answer.

The MLLM-based Refer-Planner converts each query into a
schema containing trackable subject phrases, identity and state
attributes, relational and exclusion constraints, temporal predicates,
optional target cardinality, and an explicit zero-target flag. Schema
validation rejects malformed outputs, unknown EntityBank identi-
fiers, and incomplete fields, then requests a corrected plan.
In Stage 3, the Refer-Planner performs closed-set selection over
the identifiers and temporal segments supplied by EntityBank. It
jointly checks identity, temporal, relational, cardinality, and exclu-
sion constraints, so every prediction remains grounded in supported

5
Experiments
5.1
Experimental Setup
We evaluate ReferGaussian on R4D-Bench-QA and the four-scene
4D LangSplat protocol [17]. All methods use the same scene inputs,
query annotations, and evaluation protocols. We compare with
4D LangSplat and Segment then Splat [22], the language-field and
segmentation-first baselines. ReferGaussian uses Qwen3-VL-8B [1]
as the Refer-Planner and Grounded SAM 2 [21, 32, 33] to obtain
phrase-conditioned mask tracks. The foundation models are used
without additional training, and ReferGaussian performs no scene-
specific semantic optimization.
Qwen3-VL-8B is the default planner. The planner analysis addi-
tionally evaluates Qwen3-VL-4B [1], Qwen2.5-VL-7B [2], Qwen2-
VL-7B [36], and InternVL3-8B [46] under the same pipeline.
5.2
Main Results
R4D-Bench-QA. Table 2 shows that ReferGaussian reaches 76.5%
Acc and 34.4% vIoU, surpassing both baselines on R4D-Bench-QA,
which covers temporal, multi-target, and distractor queries.
4D LangSplat protocol. Table 3 reports Acc and vIoU for the
four-scene protocol. ReferGaussian reaches 93.01% average Acc
and 62.95% vIoU. Its Acc is 2.18 points above 4D LangSplat, without
per-scene semantic fitting.

5.4
Ablation and Analysis
We analyze ReferGaussian from four perspectives: Refer-Planner
choice, Gaussian entity construction, robustness to mask-evidence
errors, and the efficiency gained through EntityBank reuse.
Refer-Planner sensitivity. Figure 7 compares five MLLMs on 4D-
varying, multi-target/reasoning, and zero-target/distractor queries.
Acc and vIoU are reported separately on HyperNeRF and Neu3D,
producing six source-category axes in each metric panel. Qwen3-
VL-8B gives the strongest overall profile with stable trends across
both scene sources.
Table 4 consolidates the functional-interface ablations. Direct
GSAM masks and framewise tracklet association isolate the 2D
evidence and temporal-association interfaces, while the Gaussian
variants test how that evidence becomes persistent 4D support. It-
erative lifting raises vIoU from 12.4 to 34.4 over one-pass voting by
revisiting support with render-back feedback. Under mask pertur-
bations, erosion, dilation, and proposal dropping retain 32.1–33.9
vIoU, remaining within 2.3 points of the original masks. Merge and
split errors fall to 19.4 and 22.7 because they change entity identity
and propagate to later constraints. The contrast separates tolerance
to local mask noise from sensitivity to incorrect entity composition.
Efficiency and reuse. EntityBank reuse cuts runtime from about
600 to 200 seconds and local MLLM calls from six to three (Figure 8)
using local inference. The initial query is dominated by mask de-
tection and entity construction, while cached queries reuse tracks,
visual evidence, and entity descriptions. The remaining work is
concentrated in query planning, semantic assignment, selection,
and final rendering.
Failure modes. Remaining failures involve crowded contact events,
ambiguous long queries, breakage, and severe occlusion. These
cases combine rapid topology changes and uncertain identity bound-
aries, motivating stronger temporal association and calibrated un-
certainty.
6
Conclusion
We formulated Referring 4D Gaussian Splatting (R4DGS), a new
task for grounding complex natural-language expressions in dy-
namic Gaussian scenes, and introduced R4D-Bench-QA to evaluate
temporal-state, multi-target, and zero-target referring. We further
presented ReferGaussian, a training-free Gaussian-to-Entity frame-
work that organizes dynamic Gaussian primitives into persistent,
language-addressable entities and performs constraint-aware refer-
ent selection through EntityBank. Experiments on R4D-Bench-QA
and the 4D LangSplat protocol demonstrate the effectiveness of
entity-level reasoning for complex spatiotemporal grounding with-
out scene-specific semantic optimization. Together, R4DGS, R4D-
Bench-QA, and ReferGaussian establish a foundation for language-
guided interaction and structured reasoning over dynamic 4D scene
representations.
