ABSTRACT
Existing geometry-aware Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) increas-
ingly rely on external 3D encoders to provide structural priors, but executing these
additional encoder paths at inference introduces substantial latency and computa-
tional overhead. We propose GeoThinker-F, an efficient framework that inter-
nalizes spatiotemporal perception by transforming causal MLLM hidden states
into predictive latent world representations. Our key insight is that intermedi-
ate semantic representations contain sufficient information to predict useful rep-
resentations of current geometry and future semantics, without requiring an ex-
act reproduction of features from a dedicated 3D encoder. To realize this idea,
GeoThinker-F adopts a decoupled two-stage training strategy. In the first stage,
the MLLM backbone is frozen while lightweight Latent Frame Prediction (LFP)
heads learn from an offline 3D geometry expert and next-frame semantic targets.
In the second stage, the external expert is removed, and the model is jointly opti-
mized to generate and selectively integrate its self-predicted priors through a spe-
cialized Spatial-Grounded Temporal (SGT) Fusion module. Extensive evaluations
show that GeoThinker-F eliminates runtime dependence on external 3D geometry
encoders while maintaining competitive performance across diverse spatial intel-
ligence benchmarks. It achieves up to a 3.6× inference speedup, reducing latency
from 1.45 s to 0.40 s while retaining strong spatial reasoning performance.

INTRODUCTION
Spatial intelligence has emerged as a central capability for Multimodal Large Language Models
(MLLMs), with broad applications in 3D scene understanding (Cai et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026b;
Cao et al., 2026; 2023), embodied AI (Zhou et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025), and autonomous decision-
making (Li et al., 2025b). To bridge the gap between 2D semantic perception and 3D structural un-
derstanding, recent geometry-aware frameworks (Zheng et al., 2025a; Fan et al., 2026; Chen et al.,
2026; Li et al., 2026a) increasingly leverage priors from external 3D geometry foundation mod-
els (Wang et al., 2025b; 2026; 2025c), either by integrating their features into the inference pipeline
or distilling their representations during training. By jointly processing multiple frames, these ge-
ometry models provide fine-grained structural cues that complement the semantic representations of
conventional visual encoders for reliable spatial relation understanding across views.
However, methods that retain a dedicated 3D geometry encoder at inference incur substantial compu-
tation, memory consumption, and synchronization latency, limiting deployment in latency-sensitive
settings. Training-time distillation (Chen et al., 2025) avoids this dependency by absorbing geomet-
ric supervision into the MLLM parameters, but does not provide a dense, frame-aligned geometry
interface for explicit routing across intermediate layers at inference. Dual-encoder architectures (Li
et al., 2026a) retain such an explicit feature interface, but at the cost of executing an external ge-
ometry branch. Our key insight is that intermediate semantic representations contain sufficient in-
formation to predict useful latent representations of current geometry and future semantics, without
exactly reproducing features from a dedicated 3D encoder. This raises a natural question:
Can an MLLM generate geometric and temporal priors from its own hidden states,
thereby eliminating runtime dependence on external geometry models?
To eliminate this runtime overhead, we introduce GeoThinker-F, an efficient framework that inter-
nalizes predictive latent world representations within the MLLM. Rather than executing a dedicated
3D geometry encoder at inference, GeoThinker-F learns to transform intermediate hidden states into
complementary representations of current geometry and future semantics. We realize this design
through a decoupled two-stage training strategy. In Stage 1 (LFP pretraining), the MLLM backbone
is frozen, while only lightweight Latent Frame Prediction (Yang et al., 2026b) (LFP) MLP heads
attached to intermediate layers are trained using latent geometry features from an offline 3D expert
and next-frame semantic features as prediction targets. In Stage 2 (self-driven tuning), the external
geometry expert is removed, and the self-predicted representations are selectively integrated into
intermediate MLLM layers through a Spatial-Grounded Temporal (SGT) Fusion module for spatial
reasoning. At inference, GeoThinker-F generates both representations endogenously within its own
latent space, eliminating runtime dependence on external geometry encoders.
Together, these components shift geometry-aware spatial reasoning from runtime external feature
extraction toward endogenous latent prediction. On the Qwen3-VL-8B (Qwen Team, 2025b) back-
bone, the complete GeoThinker-F system, combined with spatial token compression, reduces infer-
ence latency from 1.45 s to 0.40 s, yielding a 3.6× speedup while achieving a VSI-Bench score of
70.4. Across six spatial intelligence benchmarks, GeoThinker-F further reaches an average score of
69.29, demonstrating strong cross-benchmark performance without executing an external geometry
encoder at inference. By jointly predicting representations of current geometry and future semantics,
GeoThinker-F integrates spatial structure and short-horizon temporal evolution within the MLLM
latent space, providing an efficient alternative to runtime multi-encoder architectures.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
• Endogenous Predictive Latent Modeling: We propose GeoThinker-F, an efficient frame-
work that predicts complementary representations of current geometry and future semantics
from causal intermediate MLLM states. Through decoupled two-stage training, an external
3D geometry model is used only as an offline teacher, eliminating the need to execute a
dedicated geometry encoder at inference.
• Specialized Spatiotemporal Fusion: We introduce Spatial-Grounded Temporal (SGT)
Fusion, which employs separate geometry and temporal branches to selectively integrate
the self-predicted representations. A layer-interleaved injection strategy further separates
the two feature streams across intermediate MLLM layers, reducing interference during
spatial reasoning and preserving their complementary spatial cues.

RELATED WORK
2.1
SEMANTICS-DRIVEN SPATIAL REASONING
MLLMs (Qwen Team, 2025c; Gemini Team, 2023; OpenAI, 2025) have demonstrated remarkable
capabilities in general visual comprehension across both static images and video sequences. Never-
theless, recent evaluations (Yang et al., 2025a) highlight a persistent deficiency in dependable spatial
reasoning, positioning spatial intelligence as a critical hurdle in achieving human-level multi-modal
reasoning. To address this limitation, existing literature has investigated several distinct methodolo-
gies. One prominent direction involves the integration of explicit 3D information into the model
pipeline. For instance, Video-3D LLM (Zheng et al., 2025b) enriches video tokens with frame-wise
3D spatial coordinates back-projected from RGB-D data to capture location-sensitive representa-
tions. Conversely, another line of work emphasizes implicit geometric regularization within the
latent space. RoSS3D (Wang et al., 2025a) leverages cross-view and bird’s-eye-view (BEV) recon-
struction targets alongside denoising-style objectives to foster geometry-consistent feature spaces.
Parallel to architectural modifications, expanding training data has emerged as a highly potent strat-
egy. Cambrian-S (Yang et al., 2026b) curates the VSI-590K dataset to investigate the boundaries
of data scaling in spatial contexts. Similarly, SenseNova-SI (Cai et al., 2026) introduces the mas-
sive SenseNova-SI-8M dataset, achieving substantial performance leaps on both VSI-Bench and the
EASI leaderboard (Cai et al., 2025) without sacrificing general multimodal proficiency. Comple-
menting these approaches, reasoning-centric optimization seeks to fully unlock the inherent cogni-
tive power of the underlying LLM. SpatialLadder (Li et al., 2026b) reinforces complex, multi-step
spatial reasoning by utilizing reinforcement learning paired with verifiable reward signals. While
these approaches avoid persistent external encoders and preserve standard inference efficiency, they
typically acquire spatial structure indirectly through training data, auxiliary objectives, or reasoning
supervision, rather than generating dedicated geometry representations at inference. Our work in-

2.2
PRIOR-DRIVEN GEOMETRY INTEGRATION
To endow MLLMs with spatial intelligence, recent architectural innovations have explored vari-
ous pathways to incorporate 3D geometric representations from dedicated foundation models (e.g.,
VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b), π3 (Wang et al., 2026)). A prevalent paradigm introduces explicit
architectural additions to stream these geometric features directly into the MLLM pipeline. VG-
LLM (Zheng et al., 2025a) employs direct patch-level feature addition to construct geometry-
augmented visual tokens at the input layer. VLM-3R (Fan et al., 2026) concatenates enriched 3D
features with camera parameters, subsequently leveraging a cross-attention layer to allow visual
tokens to query the geometric context. Meanwhile, GS-Reasoner (Chen et al., 2026) incorporates
grounding-aware Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision to explicitly bridge the gap between object
localization and spatial cognition. G2VLM (Hu et al., 2026) introduces a Mixture-of-Transformer-
Experts (MoT) style architecture that bifurcates processing into dedicated geometric and semantic
expert modules, optimizing joint 3D reconstruction and high-level reasoning through shared self-
attention layers. Complementary to explicit streaming pipelines, another prominent direction uti-
lizes feature distillation or semantic alignment to bypass the runtime overhead of external models.
Frameworks such as 3DRS (Huang et al., 2025) and 3DThinker (Chen et al., 2025) distill 3D spatial
priors directly from external geometric models into the MLLM’s latent visual representations. While
these distillation methods eliminate the runtime geometry encoder via static supervision, they do not
expose a separate geometry representation for explicit inference-time routing (Li et al., 2026a). In
summary, methods that stream features from external geometry encoders provide explicit struc-
tural priors but incur additional runtime computation and latency. Training-time distillation avoids
this dependency by supervising compact latent representations, but typically does not provide a
dense, frame-aligned geometry interface for explicit routing across intermediate layers during infer-
ence (Chen et al., 2025). GeoThinker-F bridges this trade-off by using an offline geometry expert
for pretraining and generating geometric and temporal priors through internal prediction heads at in-
ference. These self-predicted representations are then selectively integrated through the dual-branch
SGT-Fusion module to support task-relevant spatial reasoning over multi-frame visual inputs.

METHOD
To eliminate the runtime overhead of external geometry encoding, we propose GeoThinker-F, which
introduces Latent Frame Prediction (LFP) heads at intermediate MLLM layers to predict comple-
mentary latent representations of geometry and future semantics from causal hidden states. The
external geometry encoder is used only as an offline teacher during LFP pretraining and is removed
from the subsequent tuning and inference pipelines. In the following, we first present the overall
architecture and its decoupled two-stage training strategy, and then describe how the predicted rep-
resentations are selectively integrated through the Spatial-Grounded Temporal Fusion mechanism.

3.2
ENDOGENOUS FEATURE GENERATION
The core insight of GeoThinker-F lies in transitioning from an execution-heavy external geometry
branch to an endogenous feature generation pipeline, where the MLLM acts as its own spatial-
temporal expert. To achieve this without causing representation drift or disrupting the backbone’s
native semantic understanding, we introduce a decoupled two-stage training paradigm:
Stage 1: LFP Pre-training. As illustrated in Fig. 2, Stage 1 aims to cultivate the MLLM’s internal
geometry and temporal intuition while strictly safeguarding its native multimodal capabilities. To
achieve this, we freeze the primary MLLM backbone and exclusively train lightweight LFP heads at-
tached to selected intermediate layers, thereby preventing low-level reconstruction constraints from
perturbing top-level token generation throughout the entire pretraining process.
Stage 2: Self-Driven Tuning. Transitioning to joint spatiotemporal reasoning, we completely dis-

EXPERIMENTS
In this section, we first provide implementation details in 4.1, followed by evaluation results on
spatial reasoning benchmarks in 4.2, demonstrating the effectiveness of our approach. Next, we
conduct an ablation study in 4.3 to verify the contribution of each component in GeoThinker-F.
Finally, in 4.4, we provide visualizations of our core designs to better interpret model behavior.
4.1
IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS.
Baseline. GeoThinker (Li et al., 2026a) propose active geometry integration to actively select ge-
ometry features from VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b) as demand for MLLMs, serving as our primary
baseline. (1) Vanilla regime: Following VG-LLM configuration, we utilize sampled subsets from
SPAR-7M (Zhang et al., 2025) and the LLaVA-Hound split of LLaVA-Video-178K (Zhang et al.,
2024b) for fine-tuning. We uniformly sample 8 frames per scene for consistency with the base-
line. (2) Scaled regime: To probe the performance ceiling, we scale the training set with data from
VLM-3R (Fan et al., 2026), VSI-590K (Yang et al., 2026b), MindCube (Yin et al., 2025) and Om-
niSpatial(Jia et al., 2026). We increase the sampling density to 32 frames per scene, and additionally
incorporate 430k general video samples from (Yang et al., 2026b) to strengthen video understanding.
Implementation Details. To isolate the effects of our method and pre-training data, all experiments
use the same subset of LLaVA-Hound in Vanilla regime for Stage 1 pretraining, ensuring that per-
formance differences are not attributable to unequal amounts of instruction data introduced before
SFT. To better assess the effectiveness and generalization of our design, we test GeoThinker-F across
multiple VLM backbones under different training regimes (Li et al., 2026a). For spatial reasoning,
we adopt three incremental training settings, all using a batch size of 64 and a learning rate of 1e-5.
First, following VG-LLM (Zheng et al., 2025a), the training step is set to 4,656. Next, by scaling up
the VSI-Bench instruction data, we increase the training steps to 19,315. Finally, we further incor-
porate general video data from (Yang et al., 2026b), which brings the total training steps to 28,235.
The fusion ratio ρ is set to 1 . All experiments are conducted on 16 PPU-ZW810E 96GB PPUs.

4.3
ABLATION STUDY
To evaluate the effects of LFP pretraining and the two predicted priors, we conduct a controlled
ablation study on VSI-Bench using Qwen2.5-VL-7B. All variants are trained with the same vanilla
SFT data. As reported in Table 3, the SFT baseline without LFP heads or SGT-Fusion achieves
an average accuracy of 49.36 %. Adding both prediction heads and the fusion modules without
Stage-1 LFP pretraining yields a comparable score of 49.32 %, indicating that the improvement
does not arise merely from introducing additional trainable parameters. With LFP pretraining, the
Geometry Branch and Temporal Branch individually achieve 50.17 % and 50.54 %, respectively.
Combining both branches produces the best average accuracy of 50.76 %. These results validate the
importance of Stage-1 LFP pretraining and the effectiveness of both predicted representations for
spatial reasoning within a controlled and consistent SFT training configuration.
4.4
VISUALIZATION
To qualitatively examine whether the LFP heads learn geometry-related structure from the offline
expert, we visualize the predicted geometry representations alongside their corresponding VGGT
feature targets (Wang et al., 2025b). As illustrated in Figure 4, the predicted representations consis-
tently localize the dominant foreground structure and follow its evolving position and spatial extent
across consecutive frames. Their frame-wise activation layouts correspond closely with the VGGT
targets, particularly in capturing the horse-rider foreground and its structural changes throughout
the sequence. These visualizations suggest that the LFP heads effectively learn the principal spatial
organization and frame-wise structural regularities encoded by the VGGT features.
5
CONCLUSION
In this paper, we introduced GeoThinker-F, an efficient framework for geometry-aware spatial
reasoning that replaces runtime external geometry extraction with endogenous latent prediction.
Through a decoupled two-stage training strategy, Latent Frame Prediction (LFP) heads learn from
an offline geometry expert and next-frame semantic targets to predict complementary representations
of current geometry and future semantics from intermediate MLLM states. These self-predicted rep-
resentations are selectively integrated through the Geometry-Prior and Temporal-Prior Branches of
the Spatial-Grounded Temporal (SGT) Fusion module. GeoThinker-F achieves performance com-

spatial intelligence benchmarks, GeoThinker-F reaches an average score of 69.29. When combined
with spatial token compression, the complete system reduces inference latency from 1.45 s to 0.40 s
on Qwen3-VL-8B, corresponding to a 3.6× speedup. These results demonstrate that external geom-
etry models can serve as training-time teachers, while useful geometric and temporal representations
are generated internally with substantially lower runtime overhead.

B
IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
B.1
MODEL CONFIGURATIONS
We conduct all evaluations based on the 32-frame training setting using GeoThinker-F Qwen25VL-7B
and GeoThinker-F Qwen3VL-8B in scaled regime. Below, we elaborate on the specific architectural
configurations, feature injection protocols, and optimization strategies deployed across different do-
main regimes:
Layer-Wide Interleaved Feature Injection: Unlike prior designs that adjust the injection depth
according to the training-data scale (Li et al., 2026a), GeoThinker-F applies Layer-Interleaved Fea-
ture Injection across all (ρ = 1.0) intermediate Transformer layers. Importantly, full-depth coverage
does not expose every layer to geometric features. Instead, current-geometry and future-semantic
predictions are injected into alternating layers, limiting direct geometry injection to approximately
half of the network depth. Because the temporal branch predicts next-frame semantic representations
aligned with the MLLM’s visual-semantic space, it provides a semantically compatible complement
to the geometry branch. We further assign the final injection layer to the temporal branch, keeping
the uppermost hidden states semantically anchored before language decoding. This interleaved de-
sign enables consistent spatiotemporal guidance throughout the backbone while reducing potential
interference from dense geometry injection.
Domain-Adaptive LFP Head Optimization: To balance cross-domain generalization and in-
domain adaptability, the optimization status of the Latent Frame Prediction (LFP) heads is dynami-
cally configured:
16


Preprint
• Out-of-Domain (OOD) Transfer: The LFP heads remain frozen during downstream tuning.
This preserves the geometry and temporal representations acquired during pretraining and
reduces their drift during downstream tuning.
• In-Domain (ID) Adaptation: The LFP heads are unfrozen and end-to-end fine-tuned along
with the fusion layers, allowing the heads to adaptively align with domain-specific geomet-
ric and motion distributions.
Adaptive Spatial Token Compression: To efficiently scale to long temporal horizons (e.g., 32
frames per scene) without exceeding memory boundaries, we incorporate an adaptive spatial token
compression strategy following (Li et al., 2026a). Rather than statically compressing all inputs, we
introduce a sequence-length condition:
• Short Sequences (≤8 frames): A heuristic bypass is triggered to skip spatial compression
entirely, safeguarding fine-grained visual and geometric details for sparse frame inputs.
• Long Sequences (> 8 frames): Spatial compression is activated in conjunction with Impor-
tance Gating mechanism. By dynamically filtering redundant visual tokens, IG allows the
spatial merge stride to increase from 2 to 4 while retaining geometry-relevant information.

Input-Output Asymmetry and the Prefill Bottleneck.
Achieving a holistic 3D understanding
of an unconstrained scene necessitates evaluating dense multi-view video streams to avoid missing
critical spatial cues. Following the standard evaluation protocols in VG-LLM, processing a represen-
tative 32-frame video sequence entails a substantial input context of 8, 192 tokens, which comprises
8, 064 visual tokens across the 32 frames and 128 text prompt tokens. However, the target answers
for benchmark queries are often concise single-token predictions (e.g., multiple-choice option letters
or single numerical values). Under this extreme input-output asymmetry, autoregressive decoding
latency becomes practically negligible, and total execution time is overwhelmingly dominated by
visual encoding and prior extraction during the prefill phase.
Impact of Image Count vs. Sequence Length.
A key empirical insight from comparing multi-
frame baselines in Cambrian-P variants is that the number of input visual frames exerts a far more
severe latency penalty than raw token sequence length alone.
Processing a large frame count
forces standard vision encoders or explicit geometry models (e.g., VGGT) to execute repeated high-
resolution forward passes, creating a massive pre-LLM bottleneck. Latent-token distillation models
such as 3DThinker eliminate runtime 3D encoders. Under the optimistic lower-bound convention
described in the caption, we assign 3DThinker the latency of its unaugmented backbone (0.60 s
for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 0.75 s for Qwen2.5-VL-7B), excluding potential output-length-dependent
overhead from latent-token decoding. However, these compact latent representations do not provide
a dense, frame-aligned geometry interface for explicit routing across intermediate layers. Con-
versely, explicit dual-encoder frameworks like GeoThinker suffer severe latency spikes (1.45 s to
1.69 s), limiting deployment in latency-sensitive settings.
Synergistic Acceleration via Endogenous Priors and Token Merging.
Recognizing that exter-
nal geometry encoders create an unsustainable prefill bottleneck and that dense multi-image streams
harbor substantial spatial redundancy, GeoThinker-F synergizes endogenous LFP feature generation
with spatial token compression. As shown in Table 6, on the Qwen3VL-8B backbone with 32-frame

setting, purely eliminating the external VGGT encoder slashes latency from 1.66 s down to 0.91 s
at GeoThinker-F Qwen3VL-8B-32frame w/o token merge. When combined with spatial token merging,
GeoThinker-F Qwen3VL-8B-32frame reduces total FLOPs from 113.77 T to just 43.01 T and drives end-
to-end latency down to 0.40 s, delivering a 3.6× speedup over GeoThinker (1.45 s →0.40 s) and
lower latency than the unaugmented Qwen3-VL-8B backbone (0.62 s), while retaining a competitive

To further demonstrate the efficiency advantage of GeoThinker-F in high-frequency, real-time con-

Action-Chunking and Token Setup.
Evaluations are conducted on a Qwen3-VL-4B backbone
using Optimal Fine-Tuning following StarVLA-OFT. Unlike general VLM text generation, embod-
ied VLA policies operate in a tight closed loop. In a representative setup, the model predicts a single
action chunk of 8 continuous action tokens with a compact input context of 241 tokens, which com-
prises 128 image tokens derived from 2 camera views with 64 tokens per view and 113 language
instruction tokens.
Latency Reduction in Short-Horizon Generation.
In contrast to long-text generation scenarios
where pre-processing latency can be amortized across hundreds of autoregressive decoding steps,
robotic control policies generate very short output sequences with 8 tokens. Consequently, the one-
time feature extraction and prior synthesis overhead dominates overall end-to-end execution time.
As shown in Table 8, maintaining an explicit external geometry encoder in GeoThinker introduces
a massive computational burden: extra parameters surge to 1, 705.5 M, and extra FLOPs jump by
+139.34% (3.40 T on top of the 2.44 T backbone). This heavy pre-processing pipeline causes exe-
cution latency to skyrocket from 82.0 ms of raw QwenOFT to 254.8 ms, incurring an auxiliary prior
latency overhead of 172.8 ms that severely bottlenecks real-time control frequencies.

Lightweight Endogenous Prior Generation.
By internalizing 3D perception via lightweight LFP
heads, GeoThinker-F compresses the extra parameter footprint to just 277.0 M and reduces extra
FLOPs to a negligible 0.07 T, representing a 97.94% reduction in additional compute (3.40 T →

Crucially, GeoThinker-F slashes the additional prior integration latency from 172.8 ms down to

low-latency, short-horizon embodied control, GeoThinker-F provides an architecture that unlocks
fine-grained 3D spatial intuition with virtually negligible computational overhead.

TEMPORAL BRANCH
The temporal branch aims to model transition dynamics by training the Vision-Language Model
(VLM) to predict the image hidden states of the next frame based on the current frame’s representa-
tions.
Motivation and Supervision Scheme. Instead of employing redundant pixel-level supervision,
we directly supervise predictions in the feature space. While self-supervised representation models
(e.g., V-JEPA (Assran et al., 2025)) or auxiliary temporal regularizers (e.g., Cambrian-S (Yang et al.,
2026b)) rely effectively on simple L1 loss or cosine similarity, such a single-metric objective is
insufficient for our fully supervised feature prediction target in our setting. Because image hidden
states typically possess extremely small numerical scales, lightweight or naive loss functions tend to
induce trivial shortcuts, leading the optimization toward premature false convergence. To guarantee
precise feature alignment across scale, direction, statistical distribution, and dynamic trajectory, we
propose a multi-faceted objective combining five complementary constraints.

inputs, the temporal branch is conditionally activated using an indicator-based weighting mecha-

Limitations and Failure Modes.
Despite these advantages, we observe two main limitations of

• Transient Non-Rigid Dynamics: As shown in the top row of Figure 7, when fine-grained,

DISCUSSION AND SCOPE
We use predictive world representations in a restricted operational sense: internally generated latent
representations encoding current multi-view geometry and short-horizon future visual semantics.
GeoThinker-F does not aim to construct an action-conditioned or long-horizon generative world
model. Instead, it investigates whether explicit world representations previously supplied by an ex-
ternal geometry encoder can be predicted from causal MLLM states and reused for spatial reasoning.
We distinguish GeoThinker-F from both conventional feature distillation and latent-token distilla-
tion at the level of the inference interface rather than representational capacity. Conventional feature
distillation typically absorbs teacher supervision into backbone parameters without exposing sep-
arately generated, scene-conditioned geometry priors at inference. Latent-token methods such as
3DThinker instead supervise compact geometry-related token states within an autoregressive rea-
soning trajectory, allowing geometric information to influence subsequent decoding implicitly. In
contrast, GeoThinker-F predicts dense, frame-aligned geometry and future-semantic priors at se-
lected intermediate layers and repeatedly integrates them into the backbone through an explicit fu-
sion interface. We therefore do not claim that distilled models cannot encode or dynamically utilize
geometric information. Our distinction concerns where and how geometry-related evidence is gen-
erated, exposed, and routed during inference. Accordingly, our empirical comparison characterizes
the accuracy-efficiency trade-off under the reported settings rather than establishing an inherent lim-
itation of distillation.
The reported acceleration arises from two complementary sources. On Qwen3-VL-8B, replacing
runtime external geometry extraction with endogenous prediction reduces latency from 1.66 s to
0.91 s without further token compression, corresponding to a 1.82× architectural speedup. Combin-
ing endogenous prediction with spatial token compression reduces complete-system latency from
1.45 s to 0.40 s, yielding a 3.63× end-to-end speedup. These latency values are specific to the
reported hardware and input configuration, while the corresponding FLOP and MAC reductions
provide hardware-independent evidence of lower computational cost.
GeoThinker-F trades offline teacher supervision and two-stage training for lower repeated inference
cost. It is therefore most suitable for streaming, interactive, and embodied settings where geometry
features cannot be precomputed. Explicit geometry encoders may remain preferable when maximum
metric accuracy is required, as reflected by the remaining gap on distance-centric ReVSI categories.
Our goal is not to dominate external-encoder systems under every metric, but to provide a favorable
accuracy-efficiency trade-off without runtime dependence on a separate geometry model.
