ABSTRACT
Memory for long-horizon video generation must satisfy two competing require-
ments: it must remain bounded so generation can continue indefinitely, while still
recovering what was observed at a place when the camera returns, regardless of
how long ago it was seen. Reconstruction-based memories provide spatial recall
by caching and reprojecting explicit geometry, but require growing storage and
depend on estimated 3D structure. Recurrent memories keep a constant-size state,
but retention is governed by temporal recurrence, causing old observations to fade
with time. We introduce SMART, a slot memory addressed by region tokens, which
combines bounded storage with spatially addressed recall. SMART stores content in
a constant-size slot recurrence whose writes are addressed by the observing camera
and whose reads are queried by the rendering camera, without constructing an
explicit 3D representation or maintaining a growing cache. Consequently, recall is
conditioned on inter-view geometry rather than on recency. Each read additionally
provides a confidence estimate that conditions a chunk-causal diffusion transformer,
allowing it to selectively trust retrieved content while relying on its generative prior
elsewhere. On long trajectories in Memory Maze and Minecraft, SMART maintains
high-fidelity recall far beyond its training horizon while using constant memory
and per-step cost.

INTRODUCTION
Video generative models have improved dramatically in recent years. Large scale training and
autoregressive generation have enabled models to produce increasingly long videos while following
camera and action controls with high fidelity. However, even compressed latent videos consist of
thousands of tokens, making it costly to train and condition on long sequences. Moreover, a generative
model must implicitly learn complex long-range dependencies, such as those imposed by multi-view
consistency. Even large-scale autoregressive models (Bahmani et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026b; 2024)
struggle with this: when an agent walks down a corridor and subsequently retraces its path, the
environment may no longer match its initial state. Remembering scene geometry over long horizons
therefore remains difficult even at scale. Conditioning on a persistent representation of previously
observed content can alleviate both problems. Rather than requiring the model to retain the full
history and reconstruct cross-view relationships implicitly, such a representation can summarize past
observations and provide information relevant to the current viewpoint. The central question thus
becomes, how this representation should be structured.
Two families of methods have emerged to equip video generation with memory, which differ in terms
of how memory is addressed. Explicit memory directly instantiates the underlying 3d geometry in
the form of Gaussians, point clouds or a latent 3D map, and reprojects it to the target camera to
condition the generator (Bahmani et al., 2026; Shen et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2026; Wei et al., 2026;
Zhao et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). The memory is addressed by a spatial location: content
can be retrieved according to where it was observed. Explicit methods, therefore, enforce some
level of long-term consistency by construction. However, the required storage generally grows with
the size of the explored scene unless information is explicitly pruned, and the resulting rendered
conditioning provides the generator only with limited information about which reconstructed regions
are reliable. Implicit memory, in the form of linear-time recurrent sequence models, stores the memory
within the neural architecture (Po et al., 2025; Savov et al., 2026): in language models, state-space
approaches (Gu et al., 2021; Gu & Dao, 2023; Dao & Gu, 2024), gated linear attention and slot
recurrences (Zhang et al., 2024; Du et al., 2026; Afzal et al., 2026) all compress an unbounded history
into a constant-size state, evaluated on exactly the retrieval regime that concerns us. However, such
memory schemes lack an explicit spatial addresses: information is accumulated through temporal
recurrence rather than stored according to where it was observed. Moreover, every frame updates the
entire state and decays older information, so what is retained inherently depends on recency rather
than spatial location. Old observations beyond the training horizon inevitably fade out.
We propose a conditioning mechanism that combines the complementary strengths of both approaches:
the bounded, learned representation of implicit memory and the spatial addressing of explicit geometry.
We introduce SMART (slot memory addressed by region tokens), which couples a fixed-capacity
implicit memory, in which camera pose determines where observations are written and from where
they are retrieved, with a video diffusion model conditioned on what that memory returns. Rather
than asking the model to recover an observation from a particular (past) time step, a query decodes
the memory into a coarse representation of the target view together with a confidence estimate.
This provides the generative model with a full-frame conditioning signal, preserving the convenient
interface of explicit reconstruction-based methods; but without the requirement to keep a full 3D
scene reconstruction in memory. Our contributions are:
• A fixed-capacity slot-based implicit scene memory that addresses observations by where
rather than when they were seen, enabling geometry-guided writing, retrieval, and novel-
view decoding.
• A memory-conditioned video diffusion model that takes the retrieved view as a coarse draft
and refines it into video, falling back on its generative prior where memory is uninformative.

RELATED WORK
Video diffusion and controllable generation. Diffusion models have become a strong foundation for
video generation, with large-scale architectures such as Wan (Wan et al., 2025) providing high-quality
spatiotemporal synthesis. Camera-controlled generation further conditions on viewing geometry,
using Pl¨ucker-ray representations (He et al., 2025), geometry-aware attention (Miyato et al., 2024), or
relative-camera positional encodings (Li et al., 2025; 2026). These mechanisms condition the current

step on pose, but leave open what the model retains from earlier steps.
Temporal context for video generation. Autoregressive and causal diffusion methods extend video
generation beyond a fixed temporal window, including Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) and
history-guided generation (Song et al., 2025). FramePack (Zhang et al., 2025) compresses previous
frames into a bounded context, while MilliVid (Chandratreya et al., 2026) uses hierarchical latents
and coarse-to-fine rollout to preserve long-range structure. These approaches reduce the cost of long-
horizon generation, but recall stays indexed by recency: a region observed long ago is compressed or
dropped regardless of whether the camera is about to return to it.
Explicit spatial memory. Several methods equip video generators with explicit scene represen-
tations. Gen3C (Ren et al., 2025), Lyra and Lyra 2.0 (Bahmani et al., 2026; Shen et al., 2026),
MosaicMem (Yu et al., 2026), and geometry-aware world models (Wei et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2026)
use reconstructed geometry, cached views, or spatial representations to retrieve information relevant
to the current camera. Closely related ideas also appear in reconstruction systems with persistent
or online state (Wang* et al., 2025; Wang & Agapito, 2025; Chen et al., 2026a; Zhuo et al., 2026).
Such spatial memories naturally support revisits, but tie recall to an explicit reconstruction or cache
whose representation and storage must be maintained as the explored scene grows. We instead retain
spatially relevant information in a fixed-capacity learned state.
Implicit and recurrent memory. State-space models (Gu & Dao, 2023; Dao & Gu, 2024) compress
arbitrarily long sequences into recurrent states at linear cost and have been adapted to long-context
video (Po et al., 2025). Gated-slot attention (Zhang et al., 2024) and sparse-slot memories such as
Raven (Afzal et al., 2026) partition the state into selectively updated slots, so information persists
when unrelated inputs arrive. Learned states can also support geometric queries: CUT3R (Wang*
et al., 2025) predicts pointmaps from its recurrent state at queried viewpoints. GIM-World (Wei et al.,
2026) compresses past observations into a fixed-size representation with camera-queryable geometry
supervision. Yet, CUT3R requires explicit 3D supervision and GIM-World does not maintain a
low-cost recurrent memory.
SMART focuses on geometric addressing within the recurrence itself: camera pose governs autore-
gressive memory updates and readouts. At generation time, a target-camera query decodes a coarse
full-frame draft to condition a chunk-causal video diffusion model only on the relevant information.

fixed size, ht = Atht−1 + Btut with output yt = Ctht, so that arbitrarily long histories are

state is nonetheless a decaying summary ordered by time: what can be recovered from ht about

3.2
SPATIALLY-STRUCTURED MEMORY DESIGN
The memory is a bank of M slots per head across Nh heads, written as posed latent frames arrive and
read by presenting a target camera. Its design enforces one separation: everything that decides where
content goes (keys, queries, and routing) is computed from pose alone, while what is stored, and how
strongly it overwrites, is computed from content. Pose enters as data on the addressing side, as an
absolute code matched between writer and reader, and as an operator on the content side, where the
relative camera between writer and reader transports stored values and warps the decoder’s attention.

3.3
MEMORY-CONDITIONED VIDEO GENERATION
The generator’s task is to turn the memory’s coarse draft into video. It is a diffusion transformer that
sees the memory through an interface composed of a coarse latent and its confidence mask. The
current latent, memory prediction, and its confidence mask are channelwise concatenated before
patch embedding similar to Wan et al. (2025), giving each token a spatially aligned memory estimate.

3.4
TRAINING
We train in two stages, with a frozen domain-specific autoencoder throughout (Table 4). The memory
is trained first, alone and as a plain regressor, so that its reads are verified retrieval before any generator
depends on them. The diffusion transformer is then trained on top of the frozen memory.
Memory training.
The memory is trained through a single interface: having written the observed
frames Ot into its state, it is handed one query pose and must return the latents observed there,
ˆz = fθ(zOt, πOt; πτ(t)) ≈zτ(t). Everything the memory must learn is expressed by the choice of τ
and O: revisits (Crev), answerable only by recall, co-observed novel views (Cnvs), answerable only by
fusing stored viewpoints, past (Cpast) and current frames (Ccur), and held-out spans (Cho), a quarter
of each clip excluded from the write and rendered purely from surrounding observations. Recall
and synthesis are the same read, differing only in where the target lies relative to what was written.
Reading never modifies the state, so one written prefix can be queried at arbitrarily many cameras and
all targets are supervised in parallel. The recurrence itself trains as a parallel scan, without unrolling.

The transformer is trained with the flow-matching loss (Lipman et al., 2022)

on will be self-generated and imperfect, so the clean context is sometimes noised, with the true level
stamped through the modulation. Additionally, the draft it is handed will sometimes also be wrong, so
drafts are deliberately corrupted, from single cells to the whole stream. Training details and optimizer
settings for both stages are listed in App. A.3.

EXPERIMENTS
Setup.
We evaluate on Memory Maze (Pasukonis et al., 2022; Tunyasuvunakool et al., 2020),
procedurally generated egocentric 3D mazes at 64 × 64, and Minecraft (Yan et al., 2023; Guss et al.,
2019) open-terrain trajectories at 256 × 256 resolution (Figure 8). Memory Maze is our primary
testbed where revisits are enforced by construction, the maze layout gives an unambiguous ground
truth for what should be recalled, and the low resolution makes ablations affordable. Minecraft tests
whether the same mechanism holds at scale, on higher resolution action-conditioned generation.
For Minecraft, we adopt 600-frame rollouts from the training dataset of Chandratreya et al. (2026)
and we generate 401-frame rollouts to train on Memory Maze while we evaluate at longer horizons.

Memory Maze retraces the observed trajectory in reverse, whereas Minecraft continues along the
action-conditioned path. Following the protocol of Chandratreya et al. (2026), these paths are
sampled to close loops over the context, so reconstruction metrics primarily measure recall of
observed content. The two instantiations share the interface of Section 3.3 but differ in backbone,
autoencoder, and resolution (Table 4; details in Section A.5).
Metrics.
For reconstruction we report mean per-frame PSNR, SSIM (Wang, 2004) with 11 × 11
window of σ = 1.5 and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) with AlexNet features. For generation, we report
FID (Heusel et al., 2017) (InceptionV3, 2048-D) and FVD (Unterthiner et al., 2018) (I3D, 1024-D).
4.1
IMPLEMENTATION
Memory design.
We instantiate SMART with a GSA slot recurrence (Zhang et al., 2024)
using Mamba-2 decay (Dao & Gu, 2024) per slot, adapted from Raven (Afzal et al., 2026) for
pose-addressed video memory. Full architectural and training details are given in Sections A.3
and A.4.
Generator design.
We instantiate SMART with two diffusion-transformer backbones to evaluate
the memory across different generation settings: a camera-conditioned Wan2.1-style model (Wan
et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025) on Memory Maze and an action-conditioned DiT-B (Peebles & Xie, 2023;
Chandratreya et al., 2026) on Minecraft. Both start from a chunk-causal generator without memory,
trained by us over the full clean history on Memory Maze and taken from MilliVid’s released 7-frame
rollout checkpoint on Minecraft, and are then finetuned with SMART-SM. Given an observed prefix,
the generator generates chunk by chunk along the control signal, and writes its own generations back
into memory. Full architectural details are provided in Section A.5.
4.2
3D CONSISTENT VIDEO GENERATION
Baselines.
On Memory Maze we compare against causal attention over the full history, its 100-
frame windowed variant, DFoT (Song et al., 2025), and GeoCache, our reimplementation of the
geometry-cached memory of Lyra 2.0 (Shen et al., 2026) after training faithful implementations based
on their official codebases. We additionally compare against our StateSpaceDiffuser (Savov et al.,
2026) reimplementation, a capacity-matched action-conditioned recurrent memory, which degrades
rapidly on longer trajectories (see Section A.6). On Minecraft we instead compare against released
checkpoints throughout, namely MilliVid (Chandratreya et al., 2026), the FramePack (Zhang et al.,
2025) reimplementation distributed with it, and its 7-frame full-resolution rollout, each run through
its authors’ own sampling code. Implementation details for all baselines are given in Section A.5.
Generation results.
Across both environments, SMART maintains substantially stronger long-
horizon reconstruction fidelity than temporal-context baselines while remaining markedly more
compute-efficient than the strongest long-context alternatives (Tables 1 and 2). On Memory Maze,
reconstruction quality improves up to the training horizon and degrades only gradually as the trajectory
grows longer, whereas all temporal-context baselines deteriorate more. Across the longer horizons,
SMART maintains a clear margin in reconstruction quality while requiring substantially less compute
than DFoT and GeoCache. On Minecraft, SMART achieves the highest PSNR and SSIM across all
horizons and the best LPIPS in three of four bands, while trailing MilliVid on FID and on FVD at
intermediate horizons, at roughly 2.7× lower compute. Qualitative examples are shown in Figure 6
for Minecraft and Figure 10 for Memory Maze.

CONCLUSIONS
In this work we presented SMART, a pose-addressed slot memory for long-horizon video
generation that combines constant-size recurrent storage with spatially indexed recall. By separating
pose-derived addressing from content storage, updating only the slots of the observed region, and
canonicalizing stored values across viewpoints, SMART makes retention depend on where content
was seen rather than when. The retrieved view serves as a coarse draft that a chunk-causal diffusion
model refines into video, drawing on stored scene content where it is available and on its generative

Limitations & Future Work.
A central limitation of the current design is that fixed memory
capacity bounds the spatial extent a model can retain. Although selective routing prevents information
from decaying with elapsed time, sufficiently large environments eventually force different regions to
reuse the same slots, causing previously stored content to be overwritten and forgotten. A natural
direction for future work is therefore to make the memory capacity adaptive at inference time, for
example, by allocating additional slots or memory banks online when the existing state becomes
saturated, while preserving efficient pose-based addressing and retrieval. In addition, our current
experiments use relatively modest generator backbones, scaling the approach to substantially larger
video models is an important next step to assess how the memory mechanism interacts with stronger
generative priors and higher visual fidelity.

A.1
RELATION TO LINEAR RECURRENT MODELS
Closely related to our work, we draw similarities and differences with Raven (Afzal et al., 2026),
a gated-slot attention based linear-time sequence model. We build on its state architecture where
each head keeps a fixed number of slots, updated by the gated slot attention recurrence (Zhang et al.,
2024) with a learned decay, and a router assigns every token to the slots it writes, so the state stays
constant in size however long the sequence grows. Our implementation leverages the same underlying
codebase from fla Yang & Zhang (2024) (Section A.3). The difference lies in what the memory is
asked to do. In recurrent sequence models more broadly, queries, keys, values and routing are all
projections of the current token’s hidden state, and the state is read once, to predict the next token.
A camera-conditioned generator instead has to read the scene from any viewpoint, past or future,
independently of the last frame written. We therefore derive keys, queries and routing from the
camera pose alone and values from the frame content to facilitate learning of pose-aligned memory
slots rather than content similarity-based associations. Each token writes a single slot, which remains
stable until that place is observed again, and values are stored in a camera-independent frame to be
re-posed by the target camera on read (Section A.3). Finally, the output is not a next-token prediction
but a draft of a target view, read from a state while the generator denoises and updates the state once
the chunk is complete.
A.2
DATASETS
Memory Maze.
Measuring retrieval needs trajectories that return to what they have already seen,
which the released Memory Maze episodes (Pasukonis et al., 2022) cannot guarantee: they record
an RL agent exploring freely. We therefore roll out our own evaluation episodes in the same 15×15
environment on MuJoCo (Todorov et al., 2012) and dm control (Tunyasuvunakool et al., 2020),
with an imposed three-leg structure of a forward leg of N frames, a 21-frame in-place turn, and
a return leg of N frames, so an episode is 2N+21 frames long (Figure 8a). The forward leg is a
stochastic breadth-first coverage sweep, repeatedly heading for the nearest cell the route has not yet
entered rather than following a shortest path, and the return leg then retraces the positions actually
walked. Frames are 64 × 64 RGB from a rigidly mounted first-person camera.
Each frame stores the agent position and heading. Camera extrinsics and intrinsics are derived from
the pose at load time. For training we re-render the released Memory Maze episodes (Pasukonis
et al., 2022) through the same camera (29,000 train and 1,000 evaluation), and cut into overlapping
401-frame snippets so that the environment’s goal spheres and target rings are hidden.
Minecraft.
MilliVid’s LoopCraft (Chandratreya et al., 2026) provides our training data and the
evaluation protocol we adopt, but its released test split is 1,024 frames long and our claim concerns
horizons beyond that. We therefore generate a 2,048-frame split with their released generator to
collect MineRL (Guss et al., 2019) trajectories through the pipeline of TECO (Yan et al., 2023) where
the agent moves forward or turns left/right, rendered at 512 × 512 and downsampled to 256 × 256.
Unlike our Memory Maze episodes these rollouts are continuations with no turn and no return leg,
the model being given 256 context frames and generating the remaining 1,792 (Figure 8b). We
keep Chandratreya et al. (2026)’s loop-closure bias, drawing 300 candidate action sequences per
episode, scoring each by the Chamfer distance between the planned positions of the first 256 frames
and the remainder, and replaying one sampled uniformly from the ten scoring lowest.
MilliVid’s LoopCraft stores each episode’s video, a one-hot action per frame, and the agent position
and heading read directly from the simulator. To achieve pose-conditioning, we derive the camera
poses based on the specifications of Guss et al. (2019) and agent’s pose. For training we use 601-frame
rollouts, which is sufficient for the long-horizon extrapolation we evaluate.

Memory implementation.
Our recurrent memory implementation uses the chunked-update GSA
kernels of the fla package (Yang & Zhang, 2024), inspired from Raven (Afzal et al., 2026). In
contrast to Raven, which derives keys, queries, router logits and values from the same layer input
for language modeling, our design separates the pose-derived addressing pathway (keys, queries,
router) from the content-derived value pathway, and writes 16 tokens per frame rather than one per
position based on the tokenization approach we took in Section 3.2. Routing is top-1 with the raw
sigmoid score of the winning slot as write amplitude, so the write gate is not renormalized across
slots. Read-only operations are implemented by running the recurrence with zero keys, values and
gates, which leaves the state unchanged.
Value transport.
A view depends on where it was seen from, but a memory of the scene should
not: values are therefore written in a shared camera-independent frame, the writing camera’s inverse
acting on their channels as an SE(3) transform, and re-posed by the target camera at read time (Miyato
et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025). Because the slot read is linear in the values, a value written at πτ and
read at π∗undergoes exactly the relative transform ρ(π∗)ρ(πτ)−1, as if carried directly from the old
camera to the new one. Keys are deliberately not transformed. A slot key is an exponential moving
average over many writes, so no single writing camera exists to unrotate, and the relative-camera
effect the rotation would provide already reaches the read through the values, so the matching stays
absolute by design.
VAE design.
Both autoencoders are frozen before any memory or generator is trained, and within a
dataset a single autoencoder serves every latent-space method, so that all reported pixel metrics are
measured against a common ceiling.
On Memory Maze we finetune convolutional Wan-VAE (Wan et al., 2025) and leave its architecture
untouched with 16 latent output channels and causal 3D convolutions giving 8× spatial and 4×
temporal compression. Encoder and decoder are both trained for 40,000 steps on the re-rendered
episodes of Section A.2 according to the training details of Wan et al. (2025).

DiT design.
To show that the memory is not tied to one generator, we instantiate it with two
diffusion transformers: a camera-conditioned Wan2.1-style model (Wan et al., 2025) with 250M
parameters on Memory Maze following the model size of Po et al. (2025) and MilliVid’s action-
conditioned DiT-B (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Chandratreya et al., 2026) with 130M parameters on
Minecraft. Both read the memory through channelwise concatenation onto its input channels and
every rollout step generates chunks of N=3 latent frames from a window of W=6 clean frames.
Everything else follows the respective backbone (Table 4).
Memory Maze. The backbone is a stack of 14 Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) blocks of width 1,024 with
8 heads, conditioned only through Wan’s per-token timestep modulation, without cross-attention. A
36-channel patch embedding maps each cell of the 8 × 8 latent grid to one token, 64 per frame. The
camera enters attention as PRoPE (Li et al., 2025), a parameter-free camera projection on queries
and keys on 40 head channels, while the remaining carry temporal and 2D-rotary embeddings. The
sequence is laid out as [ SINK | CONTEXT | GEN ]: frame 0 is a permanent clean sink token group,
the context rows hold the W clean frames before the chunk, and the generation rows hold the noised
chunk, which attends to the anchor, its window and itself. Thus, a generation token then attends to at
most (1 + W + N) × 64 = 640 tokens.
Minecraft. The backbone is MilliVid’s DiT-B (Chandratreya et al., 2026), 12 blocks of width 768
with 12 heads, reproduced parameter for parameter. Conditioning is per token: every token carries
its own adaptive-layer-norm modulation from its noise level, the action embedding (forward, turn
left/right) is added to that modulation, and position is an additive sinusoidal code over frame. Each
of the 256 cells of the 16 × 16 latent grid is one token, embedded by a linear map over 65 input
channels; the 33 columns beyond the latent are the memory interface and the only parameters absent
from the released checkpoint. Attention is over the W +N = 9 frames of a clip, 2,304 tokens against
the 3,840 of MilliVid or FramePack, and there is neither camera input nor classifier-free guidance.

A.4
TRAINING FORMULATION
Spatially-Structured Memory Training.
The memory is trained alone and before any generator,
as a deterministic regressor over posed latents. For each training clip (401 and 601 frames), we
pre-encode the frames with a pretrained VAE into 101 and 601 latent frames for Memory Maze
and Minecraft datasets, respectively. The camera poses are kept at the native frame rate, rebased to
E0 = I, and divided by a single scene-scale constant. At every latent position t, the model writes the
observable prefix Ot into its state, is handed one query pose πτ(t), and returns the latents observed
for the query pose, ˆzt = fθ(zOt, πOt; πτ(t)) ≈zτ(t). Reading operation never modifies the state, so
queries are executed at every position in parallel since the recurrence itself trains as a parallel scan,
without unrolling (Zhang et al., 2024).
Target selection. The target index τ(t) is drawn independently at each position. A revisit, Crev, is a
future frame whose camera lies within 0.5 world units and a camera direction with cosine similarity
larger than 0.9 that is already in the written prefix, and is answerable only by recalling that visit (10%
of targets). A fusion target, Cnvs, is a future pose that is not a near-duplicate of anything written, but
whose viewing footprint is covered by at least one written camera (45%). It is answerable only by
fusing several stored viewpoints. The current frame, Ccur, reads its own write back out, supervising
the write–read round trip end to end (2%). The remaining 42% recall a uniformly random previously
written frame (Cpast) that is re-queried.
Held-out spans. Independent of the draw above, six blocks of four consecutive latent frames, 24
of the 101, are reserved at every step and never written (Cho). Reserved frames are withheld as
revisit evidence and as fusion observers, and each claims two later positions as queries, so about half
of all queries ask for a span the memory was never shown and must render from the observations
surrounding it. These positions are scored by a plain squared error rather than the confidence-
weighted.

3D Consistent Video Generation Training.
The generator is trained after the memory module is
trained according to Sections A.4 and 3.2 and on top of it: the memory stays frozen for storing the
latent history and to condition the transformer in a causal manner. It predicts the velocity with one
noise level per chunk of N=3 latent frames and the W=6 clean frames before the chunk as context,
and the two augmentations of Section 3.4 are applied at the rates given below. Half of the clips have
each context frame noised with probability 0.5 to a level drawn from U(0, 0.3], and each draft frame
is corrupted with probability 0.08, while the whole draft is replaced by noise for 20% of clips on
Memory Maze and 5% on Minecraft, where a further 15% of clips instead have their context replaced
by noise so that the draft is the only source of information.
Memory Maze DiT training. No pretrained generator exists for Memory Maze, so we train one in two
stages, the second initialized from the first. (i) A bidirectional image-to-video model is pretrained on
101-latent-frame clips with all-to-all attention (Figure 9a) and one noise level per clip, for 50,000
steps at an effective batch of 96 on 12 RTX4090 GPUs, with a learning rate of 5e−4 and decayed
by a cosine schedule. (ii) It is then finetuned with the frozen memory: the context is cut to the
W=6 window of Figure 9c and the draft and trust channels are attached. Noise levels are drawn
logit-normally per chunk and the target is the rectified-flow velocity.
We use AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) with β = (0.9, 0.95), a weight decay of 0.01, and a
learning rate of 1e−4 decayed by a cosine schedule. The model is finetuned for 15,000 steps at an
effective batch of 40. At inference every chunk is denoised in 35 Euler steps and every generated
chunk is written back into the memory module. The Causal (Full) baseline is a separate finetune
of (i) with the full-history chunk-causal mask of Figure 9b, in which every chunk attends to the clean
ground-truth history below it, trained for ≈40,000 steps at batch 72 with a learning rate of 1e−4 and
cosine decay scheduling.
Minecraft DiT training. The generator is initialized from MilliVid’s released 7-frame full-resolution
rollout checkpoint, the Causal (7F) arm of Table 2. Only the patch embedding is widened, from
32 to 65 input channels for the memory interface. The target is the v-prediction of a shifted-cosine
variance-preserving schedule with uniformly drawn noise levels. Each step loads a full 601-frame
window, draws one boundary s ∼U[6, 598], prefills the memory with the ground-truth frames [0, s)
and reads the draft of frames [s, s+3) from that state without writing, so the draft depends only
on frames before the chunk. The trust label is the same map as for Memory Maze with constants,
α = 0.15 and ϵ = 0.007, and τmax = 0.8. We use AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) with a
weight decay of 0.01 and a learning rate of 1.5e−4. The model is trained for 60,000 steps at an
effective batch of 64 with 4 accumulation steps. At inference, the memory is prefilled with the 256
context frames and each 3-frame chunk is denoised with 50-step DDIM sampler and written back
into the memory.

A.5
3D CONSISTENT VIDEO GENERATION EVALUATION
Memory design.
The SMART-SM is instantiated identically on both datasets, as described in
Section 3.2 and Section A.3 with 8 layers of 6 heads, head dimension 64, M=16 slots per head,
top-1 routing whose sigmoid write amplitude is left unnormalized, a write gate tied to the Mamba-2
decay, the SE(3) value warp acting on all head channels as detailed in Section A.3. Keys and queries
are functions of pose alone, and each frame contributes 16 write tokens. The state is therefore
2 × L × H × M × d = 98,304 floats on both datasets and at every horizon.
Baseline Implementation Details.
Every baseline is generated based on its authors’ implementation
and scored by us after adapting for the benchmarks. On Memory Maze, the GeoCache (based on
Lyra2.0 (Shen et al., 2026)) and DFoT (Song et al., 2025) architecture, diffusion backend, sampler,
sliding-window schedule and guidance belong to the reference implementation, and evaluated on our
test dataset after training models from scratch. On Minecraft, the rollout is produced by the released
code unchanged. All methods are run on the same episodes and decoded by a shared autoencoder.
Causal DiT. On Memory Maze, Causal (Full) is the full-history causal finetune of our bidirectional
model (Section A.4), scored as an arm of its own with the same 35-step rollout and key–value cache,
and Causal (100F) is the same recipe with the clean context capped at 25 latent frames (100 pixel
frames). On Minecraft, Causal (7F) is MilliVid’s released full-resolution rollout checkpoint, run
unmodified.
DFoT. Song et al. (2025) assigns an independent noise level to every token and denoises a sliding
window under history guidance. We train it on the latents, clips and camera convention of Section A.2:
13 layers, width 1024, 8 heads and patch size 1 over the 8 × 8 latent grid, giving 249M parameters
against the 250M budget of our own generator, on 101-frame latent clips. To achieve a fair comparison,
we introduce two departures from the released code: (i) Camera conditioning is PRoPE (Li et al.,
2025), which applies a parameter-free rotation of queries and keys, replacing the reference model’s
ray-encoding pathway, (ii) the noise-level schedule draws the context–horizon split per sample rather
than at a fixed position, so every window the sampler later requests is in distribution, and History
guidance remains unmodified and the model is optimized with AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017)
at lr = 1e−4 at an effective batch size of 96 for 175,000 steps in total. We use the reference sampler
at 50 steps with stabilized history guidance at scale 4.0. Each window represents 50 latent frames and
generates 51, so DFoT carries a bounded 50-frame history rather than the whole trajectory equivalent
to ≈200 pixel frames which matches our training samples on average.
GeoCache. GeoCache is our reimplementation of the geometry-cached memory of Lyra 2.0 (Shen
et al., 2026), strictly following its official repository: every observed frame is cached with its depth
and camera, a read reprojects the cached geometry into the target camera to greedily retrieve the
frames covering the most new target pixels, and use them with FramePack-style context (Zhang et al.,
2025) both as latent slots and as depth-warped correspondence maps. We train it with the official
codebase and recipe, including its self-augmentation against exposure bias, and change only what the
maze setting requires.
The backbone is a pretrained 14-layer bidirectional model without cross-attention (292M parameters
with Lyra’s camera and correspondence encoders), as the released 14B system is untrainable at
this scale, and each step attends an anchor, six temporal and nine spatial latent frames around a
three-frame chunk, uncompressed to match our generator’s window. For 64 × 64 frames the cache
keeps full-resolution depth at every frame, and pixels without valid depth are excluded from retrieval.
So that copying from the cache is learnable, training retrieves only from frames preceding the
chunk, for three target views as at inference, without corrupting or dropping the retrieved slots;
self-augmentation is widened to the full noise range with four denoising steps, since the released
setting only presents near-clean history. The model is trained for about 78,000 iterations with a
learning rate of 1e−4, and a decaying learning rate schedule. We evaluate it with simulator depth, the
most favourable setting for an explicit cache, starting the return leg from the last observed frame and
a cache seeded with the whole forward leg and 35 denoising steps per chunk.
FramePack. Zhang et al. (2025) compresses history by representing older frames with progressively
fewer tokens. We evaluate the released checkpoint of the reimplementation distributed with Mil-
liVid (Chandratreya et al., 2026) to share its backbone, autoencoder, and sampler with other Minecraft
arms. The progressive compression on subsequent 192, 48, 12, and the most recent 3 frame groups

As reflected by the numbers in Table 1, SMART preserves
