# Compression Does Not End at Selection: Post-Selection Positional Geometry for Sparse KV Caches

Abstract 

Sparse KV compression reduces long-context inference costs by retaining selected histori-cal states, yet these states typically keep their coordinates from the full history. Under Ro-tary Position Embedding (RoPE), gaps left by evicted tokens continue to affect query–key matching, potentially limiting the use of re-tained evidence. We study post-selection posi-tional geometry on a fixed selector-produced cache, examining how positional organization affects the use of retained information and which coordinate and continuation rules im-prove answer quality. To reorganize this ge-ometry, we propose RoPERelay, which assigns compact, order-preserving RoPE coordinates, relocates cached key phases through inverse rotation and re-rotation, and synchronizes the current query and subsequent generation. It reuses existing K/V without renewed selection, historical re-prefill, or additional cache budget. Across 36 comparisons spanning two models, six sparse selectors, and three long-term mem-ory tasks, RoPERelay improves answer quality in 32 cases. For Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with BM25 on LongMemEval-S, accuracy increases from 23.6% to 35.4%. Measured complete-request time overhead is approximately 1%–3% in the evaluated configurations. These findings establish positional organization as a distinct optimization dimension for sparse KV caches after content selection.

2 Related Work 

2.1 KV Cache Compression 

Existing KV cache compression methods primarily focus on retaining historical states under a limited budget. H2O (Zhang et al., 2023), Scissorhands (Liu et al., 2023), and SnapKV (Li et al., 2024) estimate token importance using accumulated at-tention, persistence of importance, and an observa-tion window at the end of the input, respectively. Subsequent methods account for heterogeneity in budgets and semantic structure: PyramidKV (Cai et al., 2025) and Ada-KV (Feng et al., 2025) al-locate different budgets across layers or attention heads, while ChunkKV (Liu et al., 2025) retains contiguous semantic blocks to reduce the semantic fragmentation caused by token-level pruning. CaM (Zhang et al., 2024b) and RocketKV (Behnam et al., 2025) construct compressed historical representa-tions through state merging and multi-stage access, respectively. These methods optimize the selection, representation, or access set of historical informa-tion. We further ask whether the positional arrange-ment of retained states can improve the quality obtained from the same cache after selection has been fixed. 

2.2 Cache Coordinates 

RoPE (Su et al., 2024) encodes positional informa-tion through rotations of queries and keys, making attention scores dependent on their relative posi-tions. Long-context methods such as YaRN (Peng et al., 2024), LongRoPE (Ding et al., 2024), and Self-Extend (Jin et al., 2024) extend the usable con-text range through frequency scaling, interpolation, or positional mapping. Cache compression and reuse also involve posi-tion handling: InfiniPot (Kim et al., 2024) resets coordinates during continual compression. FINCH (Corallo and Papotti, 2024) assigns compact coor-dinates to states retained through prompt-guided blockwise compression. CacheFocus (Lee et al., 2025) reallocates reused document caches after layer-adaptive pruning. SelKV (Bouyahiaoui et al., 2026) investigates position reassignment alongside selective merging and attention compensation, re-porting adverse effects in a long-context retrieval setting. 2.3 Conversational Memory 

Long-term conversational systems need to store and access facts, preferences, and state updates across sessions. External-memory methods such as MemGPT (Packer et al., 2023), MemoryBank (Zhong et al., 2024) and Rhea (Hong et al., 2025) extend limited context through retrieval, summa-rization, or hierarchical storage. Recent work fur-ther moves memory management into the KV layer: EpiCache (Kim et al., 2026) uses episodic organiza-tion and fixed-budget caches for long-term conver-sation, while RocketKV-MT (Behnam et al., 2025) constructs an active cache for each query. These studies emphasize the accessibility of historical in-formation under changing queries. These memory-management methods are complementary to our approach, which organizes the coordinates at which selected KV states participate in subsequent com-putation rather than redefining memory content or access sets. 

3 Method 

RoPERelay takes a selector-produced sparse KV cache as input and optimizes its positional geome-try before question processing and generation. Inverse rotation recovers the key representation before the source-position phase was applied, and the target rotation places it at the compact coordinate. The cached states retain the representations com-puted in their original historical context. Relocat-ing key phases changes their relative rotations with current queries, which can change matching scores and the resulting attention weights. These weights aggregate the unchanged historical values, allow-ing the same retained representations to contribute differently to question processing and answer for-mation. Positional reconstruction can therefore improve how the model matches queries to existing cache states and weights their values. 

3.4 Question Continuation and Key–Query Synchronization 

Historical compaction organizes distances within the retained history, and query synchronization de-termines how current inputs connect to that history. Both enter the relative phases used for attention matching. The current question is placed directly after the aligned historical endpoints, continuing the compact coordinate sequence. Its queries thus access the relocated keys from positions in the same space. Using the shared anchor M defined in Sec-tion 3.2, a question containing Q tokens occupies positions M, . . . , M + Q − 1.Using these positions, the model runs the ques-tion forward over the relocated historical cache and updates the question KV. Logits at the final ques-tion position predict the first answer token. When that token becomes the next forward-pass input, it uses position M + Q. Subsequent generation positions increase consecutively. The position space established for question processing continues through answer generation. Newly computed question and answer keys carry phases at their assigned coordinates and enter the active cache, so later queries read both the retained history and the growing continuation within this space. Historical keys are relocated before question processing. During decoding, positions advance consecutively as new tokens arrive. 

3.5 Interface and Inference Integration 

RoPERelay interfaces with selector-produced his-torical K/V, source positions, and valid-state masks. It preserves the selected content, historical values, source temporal order, and cache budget, while reorganizing key phases and synchronizing contin-uation coordinates. This separation allows differ-ent selectors to determine what is retained, while RoPERelay determines the positional geometry through which the retained states are used. 4.1 Setup 

We evaluate RoPERelay on LongMemEval-S (Wu et al., 2025), LongMTBench+ (Pan et al., 2025), and PersonaMem-128K (Jiang et al., 2025), using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al., 2024) and Qwen2-7B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024). Qwen2 uses static YaRN to process long histories. The two QA tasks use Qwen2.5-32B judgments blinded to the method and positional condition (Qwen et al., 2025), while PersonaMem-128K uses objective multiple-choice accuracy. Each Original/RoPERelay pair shares the same selection results, historical values, source tempo-ral order, and actual cache budget. Only the post-selection coordinate mapping and phase transfor-mation differ. The main experiments use a 10% his-tory retention ratio, defined relative to the number of input history tokens. Additional experimental settings are provided in Appendix B.

Table 1 shows that an already selected historical cache retains substantial room for positional op-timization. RoPERelay improves answer quality in 32 of 36 comparisons across two models, six selectors, and three long-term memory tasks. Each gain comes from reorganizing the positions of the states supplied by the selector. These improve-ments show that post-selection geometry affects the answer quality obtained from a compressed cache. Selecting useful historical information and enabling the model to use it are complementary aspects of compression. The gains extend across distinct cache con-struction strategies. BM25 retrieves historical units, attention-based selectors retain important states, ChunkKV preserves contiguous blocks, and CaM merges historical representations. LongMTBench+ shows gains for BM25 and PyramidKV under both models, while PersonaMem-128K exhibits improvements across most selector configurations, including a 14.5-percentage-point gain for Qwen/SnapKV. 4.3 Budget Response 

We examine the quality–capacity relationship at 1%, 5%, 10%, and 20% history retention using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 200 LongMemEval-S questions (Figure 3). Each Original/RoPERelay pair uses the same selected cache. BM25 and H2O benefit throughout the tested budget range, while SnapKV and CaM show their largest gains at low retention. With BM25, RoPERelay at 5% retention outperforms Original at 20%, illustrating how positional organization can improve the quality obtained from a smaller retained set. Low retention can leave selected evi-dence widely separated in source coordinates, giv-ing compaction more room to improve matching. At higher budgets, additional states enter the com-pact space and may compete with supporting evi-dence, moderating the benefit. ChunkKV benefits mainly at intermediate bud-gets, whereas PyramidKV alternates between gains and losses. 5 Analysis and Practical Overhead 

We use coordinate interventions to study key–query coordination and source order, then relate posi-tional reconstruction to evidence matching, atten-tion allocation, and value aggregation. We also measure practical overhead. 

5.1 Key–Query Coordination 

We vary historical key and query coordinates using Qwen2-7B-Instruct, 500 LongMemEval-S records, and BM25-B8192 caches, holding historical con-tent, source order, values, and budget fixed (Fig-ure 4). Original achieves 26.4% accuracy. Key-only compacts historical keys while preserving the orig-inal question coordinates, reaching 8.4%. Sync (RoPERelay) compacts keys and synchronizes question coordinates, reaching 36.2%, a gain of 9.8 percentage points over Original. Synchronizing the query raises accuracy by 27.8 percentage points over Key-only with the same his-torical key layout. Key-only contracts the historical span but leaves the question at its full-history co-ordinates, separating the query from the compact cache. RoPERelay places both in a coordinated po-sitional space. Their joint arrangement determines the relative geometry through which the question accesses retained states. Query-only adds 2048 to question coordinates, and CommonShift adds 2048 to both historical key and question coordinates. Both achieve 27.2% ac-curacy. 6 Conclusion 

Compression does not end when historical states are selected. RoPERelay adds a positional opti-mization stage that improves how subsequent com-8putation accesses the same retained content. Ex-periments show gains across models and cache structures, more effective use of limited capacity, and changes in evidence matching and aggrega-tion, with small complete-request overhead. These findings motivate optimizing both which historical states are retained and the geometry through which subsequent computation accesses them. 

Limitations 

Our experiments cover two models in the 7B–8B range and the evaluated long-term memory and long-text tasks. Larger models, other positional en-codings, and additional tasks remain to be explored. RoPERelay currently uses a deterministic, order-preserving consecutive mapping and coordinates the historical endpoints across layers and physical KV heads through a shared query anchor. Position assignments that adapt to the task or generation state are a promising direction for further study.
